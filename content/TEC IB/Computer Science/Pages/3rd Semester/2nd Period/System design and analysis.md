# The context

New systems can be created in order to replace an inefficient system, no longer suitable for its original purpose, redundant or outdated. The purpose of creating a new system can also be to increase productivity or quality or even to minimize costs. The new system should reduce errors or flaws of the existing one.

A computer system includes [[Hardware|hardware]], [[Software|software]], people working with it or using and the immediate environment. Updating a computer system may also involve firing employees.

# Planning

Planning a system is the process of thinking about various details and organizing the activities required to achieve the desired goal. Planning should anticipate potential organizational issues such as:

- Lack of guiding organizational business strategies
- Lack of stakeholder and end-use participation
- Lack of end-user ‘ownership’ of system
- Lack of attention to required training
- Lack of attention to various organizational issues, such as organizational culture
- Lack of attention to the design of tasks and jobs, allocation of information system tasks, and others.

## Strategies for integration

- Keep both information systems, and develop them to have the same functionality (high maintenance cost).
- Select the best information systems from each company and combine them (it is very difficult for the employees to work with information systems from another company)
- Replace both information systems with a new one (increased initial cost).
- Select one company’s information systems and drop the other companies’ (policy problems)

# Different systems implementation

Business software can operate installed on a client’s infrastructure and premises or hosted on dedicated servers that belong to a company that provides such services. A locally hosted service is the most appropriate solution for larger and complex systems.

# Stakeholders

> [!important] They matter when building a system.

[[Stakeholders]] are individuals, teams, groups or organizations that have an interest in the realization of a project or might be affected by the outcome of a project.

So, any person who has interests in an existing or proposed information system can be described as a stakeholder of the system.

## End-users

Is the person who is going to use the product. A relevant stakeholder is a frequent user who identifies flaws or errors in the current system or inconveniences that they have spotted. They will be able to propose some improvements that will be crucial to the update of the system.

- Enhance organizational piece.

A successful IT system should result in a competitive advantage.

# Use of prototypes

> [!success] To prove that your system is the correct choice.

Used to demonstrate the proposed system to the client. It’s either a working or non-working preliminary version of the final product or a simple version of the final system that is used as a part of the design phase to demonstrate how the final product will work.

## Objective

- Attract the attention of the client.
- Provide just enough of the concept for the investors to decide if they want to fund the full production or not.
- Encourages active participation between users and developers.
- Gives an idea of the final product.
- Helps in the identification of the problems with the efficiency or the design.
- Increases system development speed.

# Importance of iteration

The repetition of a set of instructions for a specific number of times to get a desired result.

During the design process, the designers of the product may have to step back several times and reconsider choices they have made. Even the best designers cannot design perfect products in a single attempt, so an iterative design methodology should be adopted. As they proceed to decide how the product will finally look and function, they may stumble on several difficulties or inconsistencies that will force them to return to previous steps.
