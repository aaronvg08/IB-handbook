# Scientific method

## Bacon’s theories

- Was used in the scientific revolution (1500-1700).
- It was Bacon’s idea to keep testing old knowledge and challenge old ideals.
    - This was a very disputed idea, even though it sounds obvious today.
    - What he proposed was: mathematics, hypotheses, and technology.

## Experimentation

- Experiments were always carried out to test theories, but now it became a more layered process with multiple people involved.
- Before, experiments were very informal. It was just “let’s see what happens” when something takes place. Now, it’s a tested environment to see how a principle reacts.
- It wasn’t called science back then, it was called “experimental philosophy.”
- Instruments such as barometers were used in experiments to measure results.

## Scientific institutions

- The scientific method was used and endorsed by institutions, and that’s when it began to be widely used.
- Some of the first institutions to begin using the scientific method were: Academia del Cimento in Florence, and Royal Academy of Sciences in Paris.
- This is when knowledge and experiments began to be shared, accumulated and tested by other scientists.

## Falsifiability

> _“Falsifiability allows for scientific hypotheses and theories to be tested and potentially disproven. Thus, a hypothesis that cannot be disproven cannot be proven true either.”_  
> -Karl Popper

- Whether a claim can be considered scientific or part of scientific progress.
- It allows to refine knowledge and challenge theories.
- At the end, science doesn’t want to get absolute truths, but the best way to explain a phenomenon or a situation.

## Limits

- Science can have its limits, despite it being the best tool humanity has.
- For example, in problems regarding psychology, sometimes the scientific method fails at explaining how certain things work, like consciousness.
