# Technology

Understanding that technology is a structure that governs society, we must value two things:

- A technological object can be used for different purposes. Pharmakon concept: remedy, poison, and scapegoat.
- The historical context and technological changes change the way we are, behave, etc., as human beings.

In the current context, we find ourselves in a digitized technological environment, but that does not mean that only digital is technological. Technological objects are all the extensions of the human body: clothes, classes, cell phones, etc.

# Ellul’s Pov

> [!important] The thought that technology is a “force” that moves like a river.

- Technology develops according to its own logic, independent of human control. Technological progress follows a semi-autonomous path since it is a structure.
- He introduces the concept of the “technological bluff,”: society is led to believe in the infallibility and necessity of technological solutions. Technology could be a bias and a Kuger-Dunning effect.

# Network society

- According to Ricaurte and Catell,s we live in a “network society”. This emphasizes the transformative power of information and communication technologies.
- Nowadays, social, economic, and political activities are organized around digital networks. This shift has redefined how we interact, work, learn, and govern.
- The fact that technology is moving “faster” than we understand it, should lead us to take a critical stance on how we use it and its implications:
    - Enviromental, freedom, privacy…
- Modern technology gives and takes away.
- Not exactly the fact of having more can be better, the key is to know how and where to use technology.

# Blind faith

When we are researching, technology, offers us a wide range of complex sources, tools and methods, but “blind faith” can make us biased.

- Confirmation Bias
- Attention Bias
- Carry-over Bias
- Anchoring Bias

## Biases

- **Availability Bias**
    
    After seeing several news stories about plane crashes on social media, a person begins to believe that plane crashes are much more common than they actually are, despite statistics that shows otherwise.
    
- **Anchoring Bias**
    
    In biology research, a preliminary paper on a new chemical compound that supposedly enhances cell growth goes viral on social media. Due to its popularity, other researches design their studies based on these initial results without critically replicating them. This carry-over effect can lead to a proliferation of studies confirming the initial findings, perpetuating possible errors or biases in the scientific literature due to the influence of the majority opinion on technological platforms.
