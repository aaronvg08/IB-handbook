# Conversions

Okay, so, most times, a problem does something unexpected to trip us up and make us think. One of those things are making the units the wrong type than the one we actually need. So, let's learn how to convert units into the type we do need.

So, for example, say we have 56 km/hr, yet we need it at m/s. We convert as follows:

$$
56{\frac{km}{hrs}}\left( \frac{1000m}{1km} \right)\left( \frac{1hr}{60min} \right)\left( \frac{1min}{60s} \right)
$$
$$
=\frac{56km*1000m*1hr*1min}{1hr*60min*60s}=\frac{56(1000)}{3600}=15.55{\frac{m}{s}}
$$

> [!question]
> This is because we know that 1000 m is 1km, and 1hr is 60mins. At the end, units can cancel each other out like variables and numbers. And, multiplying by $\frac{1000m}{1km}$ is just multiplying by 1.
