Vertical throw is a type of [[Uniform Rectilinear Motion (URM)]] that deals with objects being thrown straight up with a specific velocity, reach a peak (or max point), and then fall down. 

![[vertical throw.png|center|100]]

The [[Velocity|velocity]] of an object decreases as it goes down because of gravity. As an object falls, velocity increases, but becomes negative. If you return to the same point, then it is the same, but negative.

$$
v=-u
$$

- $v$: Final [[Velocity|velocity]]
- $u$: Initial [[Velocity|velocity]]

Naturally, the instantaneous velocity at the maximum height is 0, since gravity is going against the velocity, and it stops moving completely.

>[!tip]
>When we are working with vertical throw, we can imagine the first half of the journey to be [[Uniformly Accelerated Rectilinear Motion (UARM)|UARM]] but with acceleration equal to gravity and an initial velocity. The second half is just [[Freefall|freefall]].
