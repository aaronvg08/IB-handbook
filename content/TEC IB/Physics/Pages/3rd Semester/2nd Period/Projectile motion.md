Projectile motion is a type of motion in [[Kinematics|kinematics]] that combines all elements from [[Horizontal projectile|horizontal projectile]] to [[Vertical throw|vertical throw]]. It happens where an object is launched with a specific angle onto the air, reaching a max height, and then falling into the ground again.

![[projectile motion.png|center|600]]

The horizontal element of projectile motion is just [[Uniform Rectilinear Motion (URM)|URM]], while the vertical aspect is [[Vertical throw|vertical throw]]. It is also important that, since we are launching from an angle, the [[Velocity|velocity]] in the $x$ and $y$ axis aren't the same. 
 
>[!important]
>Remember that since this is just vertical throw and horizontal projectile, at the point of max height the vertical velocity is zero.

# Splitting velocity

Since the projectile is thrown in an angle, you have to separate the magnitude of the velocity into its $x$ and $y$ counterparts. You can do so with [[Trigonometry|trigonometry]] (SOH-CAH-TOA), or just memorize the following.

$$
v_{x}=\cos \theta \cdot v
$$
$$
v_{y}=\sin \theta \cdot v
$$

- $v$: Original [[Velocity|velocity]]
- $\theta$: Angle to the horizontal
