MNCs (Multinational companies) are corporations that have their headquarters in one country and operate in at least two host countries, one of which is outside the corporation’s home country. They are defined and scaled using four metrics:

- Sales
- Profits
- Assets
- Market value

>[!question] International?
>Multinational means that you produce and do business everywhere. International means that you export products to other places, but you only work and do business in your home country.

# Reasons

Growth is a common business objective, and for a good reason, because there are many reasons why a company would want to become multinational.

- **Larger customer base**: Businesses can increase sales and revenues by expanding into countries with growing populations.
- **Brand recognition**: Increase brand recognition by expanding into different countries and cultures. This increases brand value and sales revenue.
- **Technology**: Technological progress can facilitate the growth of multinationals. For example, innovations in container shipping, improved communications with the internet, affordability of air travel, etc.
- **Tax incentives**: MNCs can bring advantages to host countries, so many countries compete to attract them. Some MNCs are offered very low taxes, such as in Bermuda, where there is a 0% tax rate (omg wtf).
	- Be careful. The practice of moving operations to another country to benefit from low taxes is a form of tax avoidance.
- **Avoiding trade barriers**: MNCs can avoid trade barriers such as tariffs, which are taxes on imports that make it difficult and expensive to trade goods across borders.
- **Costs of production**: Labor costs and costs of other factors may be lower in other countries. There is also opportunities to benefit from [[Economies of scale|economies of scale]] by selling worldwide.
- **Lack of regulation**: Lower levels of labor and environmental regulation in some countries can make it easier and less costly for businesses to make products, but this is seen as unethical (because it is).
- **Spreading risks**: If a disaster suddenly happens in a country, they can still operate in other countries without serious damage to revenue.

# Impact of MNCs to host countries

The term multinational company often has a somewhat negative connotations, and multinationals are often accused of bringing many ills of modern day life, from pollution to obesity. A common joke about MNCs is that they buy where prices are low, sell where prices are high, and try to avoid paying taxes everywhere.

However, the impact on host countries is inevitable. These businesses are so large that, no matter what, they inevitably impact the communities in which they operate. This can be both positively or negatively, depending on why they became MNCs in the first place, what they do, and how.

## Positive

- **Employment opportunities**: Wages offered may be better than those available through local employers, even though they're low by international standards. Employees can also receive training, and if they're lucky, a transfer to the company's home country.
- **Suppliers**: MNCs also provide local businesses with products, and often become direct suppliers of other businesses. 
- **Infrastructure**: Many MNCs are required to construct their own infrastructure in the host country, which can sometimes benefit the community as a whole.
- **Product choice**: Consumers are benefited by having more choices on products which may be better than those in their host country.
- **Taxes**: Governments themselves benefit from MNCs through taxes and foreign currency earnings from the initial investment and from the sales of exported goods.

## Negative

- **Bad employment**: The employment MNCs create isn't always safe, permanent, and well paid. Most jobs might also go to skilled workers from other countries rather than to domestic workers.
- **Footloose**: Companies can suddenly change their location to look for better tax benefits, low-cost labor, less regulations, etc. This means that they aren't a long-term benefit to the country, and they normally end up in countries exploiting workers instead of treating them fairly.
- **Pollution**: In a similar way to the previous points, an MNCs may not hold themselves to the standards of their home countries when it comes to environmental protection laws and pollution control. 
- **Economic harm**: The demands of MNCs for local goods and services can drive up prices. This harms consumers and local businesses, who are often left to compete for resources. They may also encourage locals to switch to international products, which hurts the country's economy.
- **Competition**: MNCs are often such strong competitors that they take business away from local companies, and in some cases, make them disappear completely. This can lead to an unhealthy dependance on a MNC, looking at you Starbucks.
