Human resource management is, by definition, the process of making the most efficient use of an organization’s employees. This involves recruiting, training, motivating and retaining the best employees. 

>[!success]
>It also means promoting a healthy, positive, inclusive culture in the workplace for the good of the employees and the business as a whole.

>[!important]
>Human resource management is a crucial part of every business. 

# Roles

Employees, or the workforce, of a business is one of the most valuable assets of any organization. HRM ensures that the business has the people it needs to meet its objectives.

>[!success]
>When HR works, a business has more efficient workforce than their competitors.

## Workforce planning

Workforce planning involves forecasting how many and what type of employees are needed now and inthe future. Two major activities are involved:

- Gathering and analyzing data about the needs of the organization. Identify HR needs that are to be addressed.
- Developing responses to the HR needs that have been identified

## Recruitment and retention

Recruitment is one of the most important roles of HRM. It is the process of seeking, finding, and hiring people for a position in an organization. Once the right people, the very best in an ideal case, are recruited, HRM works hard to keep them for as long as possible.

>[!important]
>If employees aren't motivated somehow, they often leave a business, which can be terrible if the employees were valuable.

## Training and development

Teaching employees new skills or improving the skills that they already have. Trained employees are more productive, motivated, and make a business more resilient when it faces change. HRM involves identifying and delivering training opportunities for employees in a business.

## Appraisal

Appraisal means assessing how well an employee is doing. It can take many forms, and usually involves setting up steps for the employee to improve. However, if an employee is doing very badly, employment contracts can be terminated. When the employer decides to terminate an employee's contract, it's called dismissal. 

# Influencing factors

Like a business, HRM is impacted by changes in its internal and external environments.

## Internal

- **Structure**: A structure of a business will affect human resource management. An organizational chart allows the HR department to dientify positions that should exist, and those taht shouldn't.
- **Size**: Larger organizations need to recruit, train, and appraise more workers than smaller ones. Sometimes, its better to maintain a smaller workforce, less your business becomes overstaffed.
- **Budget**: Funding for salaries and benefits affects workforce planning. In some cases,, the number of employees needed depends on demand for products or services.
- **Promotion policies**: A company's policy on promoting employees directly impacts HR. They need to keep track of employees leaving and ensure that other employees are trained so that they can be promoted when vacancies arise.
- **Flexibility**: When a business is flexible when it comes to working conditions. For instance, working hours, allowing working from home, more opportunities for teamwork, etc. HR can change these policies to make sure employees are succesful.
- **Motivation**: If your employees don't want to work, it lowers productivity and makes the HR department's job hell. Minimum wave often means minimum motivation.

## External

- **Sociocultural changes**: If people change the way they live, the values they believe, or other factors, it can affect what people do and how they choose to spend their time.
- **Technological changes**: New technologies can affect the number and types of employees that a business needs. The use of robots in manufacturing may mean that fewer employees are needed on an assembly line, if any at all. However, it means you have to hire technicians.
- **Economic changes**: If a country's GDP is declining (which is called recession), or there are high rates of unemployment, it can directly affect HR.
	- High rates of unemployment are good for a business, since it means there's an abundant supply of labor and people are desperate for a job.
	- If labor mobility is high, which is the extent to which workers can move to a new location to take a job or change careers, it can make it easier to hire employees.
	- Modern economies are seeing an increase in temporary work. If your business is inside a [[Gig economy|gig economy]], HR should adapt to it instead of resist it.
- **Legal and political changes**: Things like how laws change and who's the president at the moment can affect HRM since some policies specifically protect against discrimination, even if accidental.

# Change in the workplace

Some employees welcome change, but others will most inevitably resist change. The changes that employees may resist can include:

- Technology changes
- Adapted company culture
- Salary changes
- Etc.

## Reasons for resistance to change

- **Self-interest**: An employee resist because they simply believe that their own interest is different from the [[Business objectives|organization's objectives]], and that the change has negative consequences for them.
- **Low tolerance**: A lot of people prefer to have an environment of order and stability. Many people fear change and don't see it as an opportunity.
- **Misinformation**: If employees don't understand or have the wrong information regarding change, they may feel uneasy and try to block the change.
- **Different assessment**: An employee has a different perception of a situation and thinks that it's not necessary. Sometimes, they believe that management doesn't know what it's doing.

## Strategies to accept change

- **Participation and involvement**: Involve employees in the decission-making of a business. This includes having sessions where employees can ask questions and be a part of the process. This helps employees feel better and important.
- **Communication**: Changes need to be communicated regularly. You must keep all [[Stakeholders|stakeholders]] involved to ensure there isn't misinformation and to ensure the positive aspects of the change are considered.
- **Facilitation and support**: Most people will need support to help them cope with change, specially if it’s very big. Some employees may also need some support to deal with what's coming.
- **Negotiation**: A stick-in-carrot approach where employees are given an incentive to accept the change. If they still don't agree they can be given an ultimatum, where they either accept the carrot and stay, or leave.
- **Manipulation** (wtf??): Some employees can be invited to dialogue with management, in which they can be influence towards the change. This approach needs to be taken carefully though, since it can be unethical.
- **Coercion**: This is the last approach management should take. It basically means forcing people to accept change, else they are fired. It can be explicit, where you straight up tell the employee, or implicit, where you suggest there could be consequences for not adapting.
	- It's better to do it implicitly since it can be unethical, and in some places, illegal.
	- It's the objectively worst approach to change management and often leads to moral and reputational damage.
