The Ansoff matrix is a toolkit in the business management course that examines the potential avenues for growth based on the company's products and markets. 

It divides strategies into four:

- Market penetration
- Product development
- Market development
- Divesification

![[ansoff matrix.png|center|500]]

# Market penetration

The market penetration strategy involves selling more of the same products and services to the same customers, or at least the same types of customers. It's usually considered the least risky growth strategy as it rarely entails making large investments. 

>[!example]
>In the case of a neighborhood bakery, for example, market penetration might involve extending hours, changing pricing strategies, or using loyalty cards or other promotion strategies in an attempt to increase sales.

>[!failure]
>In many cases, market penetration doesn't allow the organization to grow and develop as fast as it would like. Maybe potential customers cannot be persuaded to buy more.

# Product development

Product development involves selling new products within the organization's existing market, often to already existing customers. It usually involves some risk since it requires more investment in time and resources.

>[!example]
>In the example of the bakery, product development might involve selling cakes, sandwiches, or beverages to complement the simple baked goods.

>[!warning]
>Efforts to sell new products may fail if managers don't understand customers' needs and expectations.

# Market development

Market development involves selling existing products to new customers. It is consider riskier than a market penetration strategy because the organization may not understand the needs of the new customers, so its offerings might not be adapted to the new market.

There are several different types of market development strategies:

- Offering a new geographic market, such as a new neighborhood, new town, or a new country.
- Selling the existing product to a new demographic group or target market.
- Begin to sell directly to individual customers and families, if the previous model was based on selling only to other businesses.

>[!example]
>In the bakery example, this might involve opening a bakery in a new location and selling the same products in the new shop as in the original one.

# Diversification

Diversification involves selling new products in a new market. This is the riskiest growth strategy, as the business is involved in activities where it may have little knowledge. There is a chance of making very costly mistakes here.

If the new activity has some similarities with the existing business, it may be considered a related diversification. Otherwise, it is called unrelated diversification.

>[!example]
>In the bakery example, this would mean selling new types of baked goods in a new geographic market or to a new target market.

>[!danger]
>Engaging in diversification that is completely unrelated to the original business is, in most cases, the riskiest growth strategy of all.
