Human resource management is, by definition, the process of making the most efficient use of an organization’s employees.

## Importance

Human resource management is a crucial part of every business. It ensures the recruitment, training, motivation and retention of the best employees. It promotes a healthy, positive, inclusive culture in the workplace for the good of the employees and the business as a whole.

# Strategic management of employees

- Increase market share.
- Attaining social objectives.
- Maintaining employments in low-income countries.

Employees are viewed as valuable assets. When HR works, a business has more efficient workforce than their competitors.

## Less management

Sometimes, operating with fewer management can work and does work at times. This saves costs from hiring managers, and makes communication easier.

# Roles

1. Develop employees’ skill to meet the future needs of the organization.
2. Create and keep loyal workforce.
3. Respond to changing external environments.

# Temporary employment

Short-term contracts are also called **“temporary employment contracts”.**

While the length varies, short-term contracts are employment agreements used when:

- There is a need to fill in for permanent employees on leave.
- Manage seasonal workload demand.
- Add expertise to a short-term project.

# Plan

A human resource plan assesses the current and future capacity of a business’s workforce and sets out actions necessary **to meet the business’s future human resource needs.**

## Internal factors

- Structure of the organization
	- The organizational structure determines the way in **which tasks** are located and also determines the coordination and supervision between employees so that the organizational aims are achieved.
- Size of the organization
	- Larger organizations need to recruit, train, and appraise more workers than smaller organizations. Consequently, more complex human resource planning is required in larger companies.
- Budget
	- The business will need to find money to pay for the positions they need. Additional funds are needed for training and professional development opportunities and employee wellbeing. Wages, salaries, training and well being.
- Promotion
	- These internal opportunities for professional growth help to retain qualified employees. A company’s policy on promoting employees internally is important in workforce planning.
- Flex time
	- Flex-time is a way of working which allows employees to fit working hours and **individual circumstances**.
- Motivation
	- The more motivated the workforce, the greater the productivity and lower the labor turnover, which makes human resource management easier.

# Gig economy

> [!important] People working in the gig economy aren’t classified as employees, but as **contractors**.

The gig economy is a labor market in which **short term contracts** or **freelance** work are very common, as opposed to permanent jobs. Sometimes, these are provided through online platforms. However, as a consequence, they dod not receive guaranteed hours of work or amounts of pay.

Many well-known businesses operate in the gig economy and employ people on flexible contracts as a means of providing price-competitive products. Businesses supplying taxi services or delivering food or parcels are a big part of the gig economy:

- Uber
- FedEx
- Deliveroo

## Benefits

From the point of view of a business, operating in the gig economy and using workers this way can be an effective means of controlling labour costs. Employing gig workers reduces labor costs, as they are only employed when required and the employer does not have to pay some associated costs: such as **holiday pay and pension costs, as the worker is not classified as an employee.**

However, the idea that temporary workers are independent freelancers instead of employees is being challenged in some countries.

## In HR planning

It helps such businesses match supply of products to demand and to avoid having expensive labor resources standing idle or not having sufficient workers to meet customers’ needs.

# Change in the workplace

Some employees welcome change, but others will most inevitably resist change.

- Technology changes.
- Adapted company culture.
- Etc.

## Reasons for resistance to change

- Prefer the current situation.
    - People just prefer the things the way they are. It’s comfortable.
- Different assessment.
    - People do not agree with the change.
    - Some may resist the change because they think it’s not the right plan.
    - They may have a better idea.
- Self interest.
    - This is when an employee believes that the company’s interests are straying away from their own interests.
- Misunderstanding.
    - People may not understand why change is needed.
    - Employees may not appreciate there is need of change and they think everything was fine the way it was before.

## Strategies

- Participation and involvement.
    - Making sure that the employees are involved in the decision-making.
- Facilitation and support.
    - Most people will need support to help them cope with change, specially if it’s very big.
- Co-option and manipulation
    - Co-option involves bringing specific individuals into roles that are part of change management, like managers.

- Negotiation & bargaining
    - The idea here is to give people who resist an incentive to change, or leave.
    - The negotiation and bargaining might involve offering better financial rewards, or an ultimatum.
- Coercion
    - Might be effective, but it damages moral and trust.
    - **Explicit**
        - This approach is very much the last resort. Basically, you accept change or you’re fired.
    - **Implicit**
        - Suggesting the likely negative consequences for the business failing to change, without making explicit threats.
