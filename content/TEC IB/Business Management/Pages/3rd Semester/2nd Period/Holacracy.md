Holacracy is a a particular form of flat (horizontal) organizational structure where teams of people self-organise as needed to complete the necessary work for the organization. Authority is distributed, and there are few managers.

- **Roles**: In a holacracy, there are roles instead of jobs. So, people can hold multiple roles in a holacracy. Roles tend to have a name attached to them, a purpose, and domains over which they have influence.
- **Circle structure**: Roles in a holacracy self-organise into circles, or teams of people. These may be organized differently from traditional departments.
- **Organic development**: Circles come and go as needed when employees sense changes in the business and its needs. People’s work may evolve in several circles, and there isn't a tight order involved.
- **Self-organized**: Circles are not told how to do their work by managers. They are guided by a general set of the [[Business objectives|organization's objectives]], but within that they can determine how to do their work.
- **Flexible leadership**: Leadership positions are not defined in holacracy, but leadership roles emerge and then disappear in teams as needed and depending on the context.

>[!success]
>This business structure is good because it gives employees a whole deal of freedom and the organization has the power to adapt to almost anything.

>[!warning]
>This business structure doesn't always work because it needs a lot of maturity from employees, and you have little to no control over what is going on.
