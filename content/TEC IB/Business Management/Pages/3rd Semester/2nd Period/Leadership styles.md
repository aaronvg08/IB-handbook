You can't lead a bakery the same way that you lead a military. Obviously, there has to be some differences in the way you approach people. The IB recognizes the following leadership styles:

- **Autocratic**. This leadership style is characterised by a domineering and possibly tyrannical approach. It is strong and rule-oriented.
- **Paternalistic**. With this leadership style, the organisation’s interests and its employees are looked after as if they were family.
- **Laissez-faire**. From the French term which can be translated roughly as ‘hands-off’, this leadership style is characterised by a lack of interference from the leader of an organisation.
- **Democratic**. This leadership style values inclusiveness and employees’ input. It is the opposite of autocratic.
- **Situational**. This leadership style is not easy to define. The best way to think of it is as a style that someone adopts for a particular situation.

# Autocratic

This leadership style is characterized by a domineering and possibly **tyrannical approach.** It is strong and rule-oriented.

- Is a style of leadership where the leader concentrates virtually all decision-making in their own hands.
- In a work environment, the autocratic leader dictates orders, and employees follow these orders without question.
- ‘Follow orders’ could be the mantra of this type of leader.
- Is more common in large organizations, such as in the military forces, where everyone is required to do exactly as they are told.

## Disadvantages

- Employees’ ideas and feedback may not be sought or welcomed.
- Innovative ideas and creativity may be stifled as there is no mechanism for employees to voice or act on their ideas.
- This style of leadership depends on very closely defined instructions and orders.
    - and there will be problems if there is scope for different interpretations.

# Paternalistic

A style of leadership in which people are treated like they’re family.

- It creates a sense of belonging, which leads to loyalty and length of service.
- Decisions are made with employee happiness in mind, not just profits.
- This understandably leads to trusting familial working conditions that can be beneficial for productivity.

## Disadvantages

- When there is a deep loyalty to the company, both the leader and the employee sometimes fail to see that the best interests of the company are not being addressed.
- If the company experiences hard times, difficult decisions will have to be made and employees let go.
    - This can cause great disruption to a business.

# Laissez-Faire

From the French term which can be translated roughly as ‘hands-off’, this leadership style is characterized by a lack of interference from the leader.

- It gives employees a great deal of freedom to do whatever they want, which makes them more motivated as a result
- It makes employees more feel confident and empowered and lets them take part on the business' decision-making more easily.
- It can build great energy and drive among employees because the leader is demonstrating implicit trust.

## Disadvantages

- If a business recruits an inexperienced person who does not know how to achieve the objective, they will feel lost when nobody tells them what to do.
- You have no control over anything.

> [!warning] 
> This style of leadership will not suit all employees. Competent and confident employees are necessary for this model to work.

# Democratic

Is a style of leadership whereby the leader actively seeks input from members of the organization and encourages their participation in decision-making.

- While an autocratic leader aims to increase the distance between the executives and the employees, a democratic leader tends to remove this distance.
- It can boost motivation because employees feel involved and looked after.
- It involves employees and it's possible that they actually have good ideas and can help the business more.

## Disadvantages

- It can be hard to maintain throughout the business as everyone has varying degrees of ability to lead in this manner.
- Not great in times of crises.
- Time is money, and democratic leadership and decision-making may take more time
- That could be very costly in businesses that have time-sensitive issues to resolve.

# Situational

Depends on the situation. A style that adapts itself to the circumstances. A situational leader adopts the right approach for the place, time and people involved.

>[!success]
>This leadership style is useful when leading a large organisation with diverse need s.

# Which ones to use?

- **To achieve a result or manage a crisis**: Autocratic
- **To reach an objective**: Paternalistic
- **Let employees make mistakes and learn from them**: Laissez-Faire
- **Ask employees to take part in decision-making**: Democratic
