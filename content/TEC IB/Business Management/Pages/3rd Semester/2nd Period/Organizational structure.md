The structure of each business will depend on the objectives of the organization. Every organization has a specific structure that determines the place and the role of the employees in the organization, the levels of hierarchy, the authority in the organization and the lines of communications.

# Terminology

## Delegation

> [!important] If you delegate everything you lose control, so be careful.

Is the passing down of authority through and organization.

|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|---|---|
|It can be motivating.|Costs of training.|
|Can reduce the workloads of manages.|Managers may be unwilling to pass control to Jr employees.|
|Improves the skills of junior employees.|Not recommended during crises.|

## Chain of command

Is the line of communication and authority existing within a business. Thus, a shop-floor worker reports to a supervisor, who is responsible to a departmental manager, and so on.

## Centralization and decentralization

Centralized are where the managers hold the greater decision making power, and decentralized is when lower-ranked employees also have power.

## Levels of hierarchy

Refers to the number of layers of authority within an organization. That is, the number of layers that exist between the Chief Executive and a shop-floor employee.

**Tall/vertical or flat/horizontal.**

## Span of control

The number of employees directly responsible to a manager. A narrow span of control has less managers managing less employees, while a wide span of control is many people responding to only one manager.

## Bureaucracy

> [!important] For example, hiring employees requires so many documents.

Is a system under which an organization uses **complex rules and procedures** which can cause slow decision-making and may reduce its efficiency.

## Delayering

Is a reduction in the levels of hierarchy within an organizational structure. **Knowledge management** is the process of identifying, maintaining, and effectively using an organization’s resources of knowledge.

## Matrix structure

A matrix organization has a structure in which team members report to multiple managers. It looks like a matrix board, which is why its called a matrix structure.

# External influences of HR planning

## Demographic change

- Demography is the study of human population.
- The size and make-up of a population can have important implications for planning future workforces.
- The structure of the global population is forecast to undergo substantial changes.

# The Shamrock Structure

- Core employees
    - Permanent staff who fulfill the business’s core mission
- Contract workers
    - Subcontracted specialist for non-central functions
- Temporary workers
    - Flexible workforce.
- Adaptability
    - Allows businesses to quickly adapt their workforce.

# Core workers

- These are technicians, senior managers and professionals.
- The skills of these employees are vital to the organization.

# Contract workers

- These contract workers are not employed by the organization and have specialists skills needed for particular projects.
- Their skills are not always needed by the organization nor essential for its success.

# Peripheral workers

- This is the largest element of the workforce and its proportion has increased over time.
- They perform routine tasks which may require relatively few skills.
- They are normally part-time and may not have guaranteed hours of work each week.
- This group is usually rewarded for the hours they work, which can be irregular and changeable.
