# Organizational charts

An organizational chart is is a graphical illustration of relationships between the departments and people in an organisation. They are used to visually represent the internal structure of an organization.

There are multiple types:

- Tall or vertical
- Flat or horizontal
- By product or by function or by region

## By product

With an organizational structure by produt, the business is organised into departments or divisions. Each department focuses on a different product. Employees are assigned to the product or customer to which their work relates.

- Such a structure can encourage competition between departments to produce a more viable product. 
- However there functions may be duplicated and there is no central control over each separate division.


![[organization by product.png|center|600]]

## By function

An organizational structure by function is common, since the organization is divided into smaller groups based on specialized functional areas, such as marketing, production, finance and IT.

- Businesses that are structured in this way operate well in stable environments where changes to business strategies do not happen often. 
- The level of bureaucracy in this organizational structure makes it difficult for organizations to respond to changes in the market quickly.

![[organization by function.png|center|600]]

## By region

Organizational structures by region is common in organizations that work in multiple parts, such as [[Multinational companies (MNCs)|multinational companies]]. 

- The organization’s offices in each region can operate individually while following company policies and values .
- The drawback of such organizational structure is that decision-making can become decentralized easily as the regional units have greater autonomy.

![[organization by region.png|center|600]]
