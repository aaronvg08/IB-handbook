Handy's is an important theory of [[Organizational culture|organizational culture]] that states that different types of cultures are necessary for the success of different organizations. He associated each type of culture with one of the Greek gods, and a specific dynamic.

# Power culture

- An individual or a selected group of people makes decisions for the organization. 
- Communication is highly centralized. 
- Results are the most crucial factor in assessing an employee’s performance. 
- Leaders tend to be autocratic.

>[!note]
>Charles Handy described power culture as a spider’s web. Just like a web becomes powerless without the spider, the organization comes to a standstill without the decision-makers

>[!important]
>Power culture is related to the god Zeus.

# Role culture

- Is defined by clear rules and hierarchies for the smooth operation of an organization.
- People have power in role cultures through their position, rather than because of their own qualities.
- An organization with this type of culture tends to be traditional and stable.
- Organizations with a role culture tend to be [[Bureaucracy|bureaucratic]].

>[!note]
>Handy used an image of a temple to describe role culture. A temple building is often old, stable and established, and is more likely to withstand temporary natural disasters.

>[!important]
>Role culture is related to the god Apollo.

# Task culture

- Supports dynamic, innovative and flexible companies by giving power to experts within a group.
- Companies with a [[Matrix structure|matrix structure]] often exhibit this type of culture.
- The roles within the organization are often flexible, and employees are expected to take various positions according to the requirements of the company and their own skill sets.
- Productive teamwork is valued and helps get the work done. 
- Teams of employees work mostly independently and are expected to solve problems.

>[!note]
>Handy used an image of a net to describe the task culture. Like a net, the strength of a task culture lies in all its strands and connections. Many IT companies have this culture.

>[!important]
>Task culture is related to the goddess Athena.


# Person culture (existential culture)

- Values every person as an expert and relies on their experiences to operate the business.
- Businesses with a person culture tend to be employee-centric and employees often have a similar level of knowledge and expertise.
- The business organization is considered a way for them to carry out their individual work.
- Encourages a free, clutterless two-way communication between employees and managers.

>[!note]
>Handy used an image of a constellation of stars, where each star represents a valued individual. The contribution of each is considered important and unique, as it shapes the collective ethos of the company.

>[!important]
>Person culture is related to the god Dionysius.
