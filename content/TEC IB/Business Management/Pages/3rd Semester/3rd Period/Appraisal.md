Appraisal in [[Introduction to human resource management (HRM)|human resource management]] is the way an employer reviews or rates the performance of an employee. The employee and the manager meet to discuss how the employee is doing, identifying any areas for improvement and any professional development or training needs.

Appraisal is done for multiple reasons.

- Help improve the employee's performance
- Provide feedback to the employee
- Identify any training needs
- Improve [[Motivation and demotivation|motivation]] and job satisfaction
- Identify potential for promotion
- Help decide on any salary increase

However, a problem with appraisal is that some employees may hesitate to discuss nay problems because they think it will jeopardize their salary or any potential promotions. 

# Methods

There are multiple appraisal methods used, each with a different aim.

- **Formative**: The employee gets appraisal on an ongoing basis, even while training. There is constant feedback and regulation of progress.
- **Summative**: The appraisal is only done at the end of a period or project, evaluating the overall performance of the employee to deliver a final product.
- **360-degree**: Done with surveys to gather information about an employee's performance from different people, and not only from a manager or a single person.
- **Self-appraisal**: The employee assesses their own performance. This is normally done using preset criteria, and helps the employee prepare for the discussion with the manager, though it rarely replaces it.
