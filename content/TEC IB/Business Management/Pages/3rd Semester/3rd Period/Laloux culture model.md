The Laloux culture model is a type of [[Organizational culture|cultural theory]] that suggests five types of organizational cultures, which Laloux associated with levels and colors. Each organizational culture was linked to a leadership style or management philosophy.

# Red organizations (Level 1)

- Red organizations have highly concentrated power in one or a few people. 
- Leaders maintain power through fear. 
- This type of organization can be very effective in chaotic or crisis environments.
- This type of culture might be seen in gangs or the mafia.

# Amber organizations (Level 2)

- Amber organizations are like military organizations. 
- They have a strict hierarchy with clear roles and chains of command. 
- This type of organization works well in stable and predictable environments. 
- Examples include religious organizations, state schools and many government organizations.

# Orange organizations (Level 3)

- Orange organizations are like machines. 
- They tend to be oriented on results and have results-oriented reward systems. 
- Orange business organizations are also competitive and growth-oriented. 
- Examples of Orange organizations include large state universities and many large corporations.

# Green organizations (Level 4)

- Green organizations are like families. 
- They motivate those in the organization through a shared purpose and work on creating value for a wide range of stakeholders. However, there is still a hierarchy in Green organizations. 
- Leaders in Green organizations serve the employees and there is significant employee engagement. 
- Many for-profit and non-profit social enterprises can be considered Green organizations.

# Teal organizations (Level 5)

- Teal organizations are like living organisms that adapt and evolve over time.
- Power is decentralized into teams that are flexible and autonomous.
- Employees are self-managed and people are trusted to make significant decisions.
- Organizations using [[Holacracy|holacracy]]  are usually considered Teal organizations.

>[!note]
>These cultures go from levels, and Laloux considers that Level 1 organizations are not very evolved while Level 5 organizations are highly evolved. This does mean that he considers some cultures better than others.
