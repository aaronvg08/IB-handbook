Recruitment in [[Introduction to human resource management (HRM)|human resource management]] involves seeking, finding, and hiring people for a position in a business. It is a very important process since, without the right people, the business is unlikely to succeed.

It's important that the manager identifies the right employees because if an employee is hired and they don't like the environment or are unable to cope, they may leave shortly after joining, which increases costs for the company, who then has to recruit and hire again.

# Process

The process of recruitment are as follows.

- **Job analysis**: HR studies what the job entails, what an employee does, details, training, and tasks needed, etc. This is so that the manager understands what kind of people this specific role or position needs.
- **Job description**: The manager writes a description of the job so that potential employees know what will be expected from them. Things such as job title, context, tasks, etc.
- **Person specification**: To define the qualities fo the individual needed such as qualifications, experience, and so on. It is good to filter out people who don't have an essential skill that is needed, or if the work is unsuitable for them.
- **Job evaluation**: An assessment of the value of the job in relation to other jobs and the competition so that the rewards and remuneration can reflect its value.
- **Job advertisement**: Using methods to let people know about the job opening. It can be advertised to internal employees, or to new recruits. It can be done through social media, newspapers, employment agencies, and other mediums.
- **Selection**: The manager then decides who, out of all the candidates, is offered the position. This is usually the longer process and more formal for a senior management position.

The entire process also involves an application in the form of a curriculum vitae or resume, an interview, testing of certain knowledge or skills, and the job offer in which the most successful candidates are offered an employment contract.

# Internal recruitment

Internal recruitment means that a job vacancy is filled by promoting an existing employee rather than hiring a person from outside the business.

| Advantages                                                                                               | Disadvantages                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The existing employee is already familiar with the business and its [[Business objectives\|objectives]]. | The number of potential recruits is limited to the employees already in the business. |
| The business is already familiar with the employee and may know them well.                               | If a current employee is selected, then their own position is now vacant.             |
| The recruitment process can be shortened, lowering costs.                                                | A current employee may not bring new ideas into the business.                         |
| Internal recruitment motivates other employees and lets them know they can climb the corporate ladder.   | Other employees who are not selected from the position may become demotivated.        |

# External recruitment

External recruitment is when a business fills a job vacancy by recruiting an employee from outside the business. This usually happens because the company needs certain skills that it lacks in its current employees.

| Advantages                                                                                | Disadvantages                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The number of potential recruits is greater than for internal recruitment.                | Candidates from outside the business will take longer to know the processes and culture.   |
| The business doesn't need to fill another vancany.                                        | It's difficult to know how well a new person from outside will perform.                    |
| Hiring an employee from outside may bring new ideas into the business.                    | The recruitment process is longer and more expensive.                                      |
| Hiring from outside the organization may avoid internal disputes over hiring from within. | Existing staff may become demotivated if they think they can't climb the corporate ladder. |
