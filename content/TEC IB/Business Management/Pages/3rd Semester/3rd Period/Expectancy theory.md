Expectancy theory is a [[Motivation and demotivation|motivational theory]] created by Victor Vroom in 1964. It states that human behavior is motivated by anticipated results or consequences. People will be motivated to perform if they think that their performance will be recognized and appropriately rewarded.

This theory is analyzed in three different parts:

- **Expectancy**. This is an employee’s belief that increased efforts will result in better performance.
- **Instrumentality**. This is an employee’s belief that improved performance will result in a positive outcome for the employee.
- **Valence**. This is the value that the employee places on the outcome they expect.

Therefore, expectancy theory states that businesses need to closely align performance and expected outcomes (rewards) to motivate employees.
