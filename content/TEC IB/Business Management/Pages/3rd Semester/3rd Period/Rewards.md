When your employees are doing well, it's a good [[Motivation and demotivation|motivator]] to reward them somehow. Rewards are also a common feature in a lot of motivation theories, which can make an employee feel proud of their work.

# Financial

Financial rewards involve some monetary compensation for work. There are sever types of financial rewards that a business can offer to its employees.

- **Salary**: A monetary payment for work over time, usually one year, paid throughout a specific period, such as monthly. This is regardless of performance. However, overtime work may be paid extra if stated in the contact.
- **Wage**: Employees are paid a weekly sum based on number of hours, or they are paid based on the number of units or 'pieces' that they make. Either way, it depends on how much the employee works.
- **Commission**: Salespeople are paid a percentage of the total they sell. This can motivate the employee to sell more products, and it means that if they sell none, there is no money.
- **Performance-related pay**: Employees receive a bonus in addition to their normal pay if they do well. This greatly motivates employees and is appropriate when you can quantify the employee's extra efforts.
- **Profit-related pay**: In addition to the normal pay, employees recieve a percentage of the profits that the company makes. This is usually paid once a year, and can strengthen employees' commitment and loyalty to the organization.
- **Employee share ownership**: Employees are given shares of the organization as a reward, or they can buy shares at discounted prices. Through this, employees become shareholders of the business.
- **Fringe payments**: Rewards given to the employee in addition to their pay and can include benefits such as a company car, housing, school allowance, free meals, gym memberships, and so on.

# Non-financial

Money isn't everything, or so they say. These are methods of recognizing an employee's efforts and improving motivation without necessarily spending money on the employee.

- **Job enrichment**: Employee's jobs are made more complex. It can be motivating since some people like to improve their skills and take on new challenges related to the job and their position.
- **Job rotation**: Employees change jobs for a period of time in order to learn a new skill. It adds variety to an employee's job and increases their skills, which is motivating. It also makes the business' employees more flexible.
- **Job enlargement**: Additional tasks are added to an employee's job. These are usually at the same lever as the employee's current skill set. It can be motivating since an employee may feel like they are being trusted more.
- **Empowerment**: Giving employees greater responsibility in choosing how to do their job and making decisions. It creates a sense of trust from management and a sese of accomplishment for the employee.
- **Purpose**: The job seeks to make a positive contribution to the world for the greater good. If a job has greater meaning than just money, it can improve their intrinsic motivation and make them more productive, as well as just happier.
- **Teamwork**: If an employee is given more opportunities to work in teams and have friendships with other employees, it can create strong ties and make the employees happier and more productive.
