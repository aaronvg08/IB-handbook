Labor turnover is the percentage of employees leaving the business within a period of time. The formula for labor turnover can be seen below.

$$
\text{Labor turnover rate}=\frac{\text{Number of employees leaving}}{\text{Number of employees in the business}}\times 100
$$

>[!failure]
>The higher the rate, the more employees leaving, and it is worse for a company.

# Causes

In times of hardship for a business, it may have to leave some workers go. The cost for replacing employees, specially talented ones, is huge. Paying attention to labor turnover and why employees leave is essential for a business to work.

## Avoidable

- **Dissatisfaction with compensation**: Employees feel like they are underpaid and leave when another organization offers a better salary or better rewards.
- **Poor physical working environment**: If the physical working environment is poor, employees may feel dissatisfied and go look for better opportunities elsewhere.
- **Toxic work culture**: Some businesses have a work culture that is degrading, inhospitable to certain groups, or corrupt. Employees may leave to find better places.
- **Job dissatisfaction**: If an employee is doing a job that doesn't correspond to their abilities, qualifications, or takes too much time, they may leave.
- **Human resources policies**: Some organizations have autocratic policies that have little scope for promotion or [[Delegation|delegated]] decision-making. This is inherently demotivating to employees, who may look for a workplace that is more flexible.

## Unavoidable

- **Family circumstances**: Employees may leave their job because of family circumstances such as marriage, birth of children, or moving to an area where there are better schools for their children.
- **Physical reasons**: Employees may find that their physical condition no longer allows them to work, and they may leave because of it.
- **Retirement**: People stop working and retire when they reach a certain age.
- **Dismissal**: If an employee is fired, most of the time, it is not the organization's fault.
- **Redundancy**: If a job no longer exists or is no longer needed, then the company can't do anything but let a worker go.
