Segmentation in [[Marketing planning|marketing planning]] is involves splitting a population into groups of people with similar needs or characteristics. 

Some advantages of segmentation include:

- Identify gaps and opportunities in markets
- Design suitable products for specific groups in order to increase sales and profits
- Reduce the waste of resources
- Diversify and spread risks targeting different consumer segments

# Geographic segmentation

- **Country**: Segmenting consumers by country involves recognizing different languages and cultures. This is specially relevant to [[Multinational companies (MNCs)|multinational companies]].
- **Region and continent**: Sometimes, businesses target specific regions and continents instead of countries, since their needs or specific cultural characteristics may be different.
- **State and country region**: Regions within a country are populated by different groups. Most countries can be split by geographic or linguistic characteristics.
- **Urban and rural settings**: Businesses will want to differentiate between these two settings because consumers in rural areas and urban areas may have different incomes, family life and availability of transport.

# Demographic segmentation

- **Age**: In almost every market, people’s tastes change as they get older. Therefore, it makes sense for many companies to segment their markets by age. This may involve generations such as Gen Z or Millennials, too.
- **Gender**: Certain genders have specific needs and some products are directly aimed at them, which is why gender segmentation can be useful. However, there has been a pushback against what some people perceive to be stereotyping.
- **Socio-economic group**: Socio-economic segmentation divides the population using the interlinked factors of occupation, income and level of education.

# Psychographic segmentation

Psychographic segmentation is different from other types of segmentation since it divides the population according to lifestyle and personal interests. This usually leads to targeted direct marketing campaigns where customers are offered goods that marketers believe they will be interested in.

>[!note]
>This is also called profiling, too.
