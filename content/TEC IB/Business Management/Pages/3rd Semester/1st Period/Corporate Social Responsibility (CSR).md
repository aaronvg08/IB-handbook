The definition of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) has changed over time. However, it means the ways that businesses actively seek ways to improve society and the environment through core business activities and business designs. CSR includes companies engaging in environmental preservation efforts, ethical labor practices, philanthropy, and promoting volunteering.

# Benefits

Benefiting society and lessening the negative impacts on the environment are among the main benefits and goals of CSR. Consumers are increasingly looking to buy goods and services from socially responsible companies and this can have a positive impact on their bottom lines.

> [!Success] 
> People are willing to pay more for something that is sustainable, specially in this day and age.

# Drawbacks

Sometimes, having CSR can be a bad thing. Businesses may find it difficult to change the culture of the organization to focus on CSR. All the stakeholders may be entrenched in certain ways of doing things and it will take energy and vision to bring about change. CSR can sometimes also increase [[Cost|costs]] for both the company and consumer, and if the company doesn't follow through on its CSR promises, it can endure reputational damage.

>[!note]
>It's still a good thing, it's just hard to make it happen, and hard to maintain all throughout.
