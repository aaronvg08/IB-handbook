# Growth

Growth is a common business objective.

> [!important] Growing a business means expanding the business.

## Sales revenue

Money earned from selling the product. It is obtained like so:

$$Revenue=O \cdot Price$$

Where $O$ is the number of units you sold.

## Market share

The percentage of a given market. For example, if you sell cars, you could have 20% of the market share.

## Profit

Money left after all other costs and expenses. Whenever you have a company, you have to pay taxes, to your employees, etc. After everything has taken care off, all the money left is called profit.

## Impact

Positive or otherwise social and environmental consequences.

## Internal growth

This is relatively slow.

### Organic growth

- Try to grow sales of its existing products in its existing markets.
- Develop new products for its customers
- Find new markets where it can sell its existing products.

## External growth

Faster than internal growth. Allows for more tactics.

### Merging

Merging is when two companies share the same stakeholders, and they generally work together.

### Takeover & acquisition

- **Acquisition** is when a company buys another one. This is normally because the CEO doesn’t want to manage the company any longer.
- **Takeover** is when a company buys more than 50% of a company’s share, and takes over the company without the will of the company. Very aggressive.

### Franchises

A franchise occurs when a franchisor sells the right to use or sell his or her products to a franchise. The catch is that you have to pay royalties to the person.

### Joint venture

Occurs when two or more businesses set up a new business with its own legal identity to collaborate on specific activities. Similar to merging, but a completely new company is created.

> [!important] If two companies have a baby

### Strategic alliances

Occurs when two or more businesses collaborate in specific activities but remain fully independent of each other.

> [!important] Like how coca-cola collabs with the movies but they’re both separate entities.

# Economies of scale

Economy of scale refers to a situation where the unit (average) cost of production decreases as the level of output increases. Economies of scale can be either internal or external.

## Internal

Can be achieved when we increase output. It may be because we **purchased economies of scale**, which means buying inputs at a lower cost and bigger size.

> [!important] Buying a dozen eggs and a thousand eggs. A thousand costs less!

We can also do **marketing economies of scale**, which is when the cost of a marketing campaign is spread over a larger quantity of output, thus lowering the average cost of the campaign.

**Managerial economies of scale** occur when the cost of hiring a manager is spread over a larger output. So, the more people working a business, the better.

**Technical economies of scale** is when a business invests in, for example, better machinery to manufacture products, and decreases costs of production.

Finally, **financial economies of scale** is when a business takes out a large loan. Large loans have less interest than smaller loans, so this is better for us in the long-term.

## External

External economies of scale is stuff like:

- Innovation
- Infrastructure
- Specialization

# Diseconomies of scale

Companies, or some of their operations, sometimes get so big that they become less efficient, leading to an increase in unit costs as output increases. So, growing as a company is not so good sometimes.

## Internal

- Loss of control
- Managerial issues
- Lack of communication
- Increase in size of workforce

## External

- Limited natural resources
- Limited infrastructure
- Increased regulation
- Pollution

# Small business

> [!important] Some companies decide to stay small.

Many small businesses are comfortable with the amount of profit they are making and may not want to grow. This isn’t necessarily bad for the business.

## Advantages

- Avoiding risk, maintaining control.
- Small market size
- Limited sources of finance

- Sustainability
- Strong social networks

## Disadvantages

- Tend to have higher costs of production.
- They cannot take advantage of the [[Growth and Evolution#Economies of scale|economics of scale]] available to larger businesses.
- Lower profits.
- Access to finance can be difficult.
- Trouble recruiting and retaining skilled workers.
- Larger businesses may be able to offer higher salaries, greater benefits and more professional development.

# Generative/regenerative businesses

Some businesses work to grow and develop ecosystems in which they operate by being generous with those in their networks.

## Characteristics

- They share their know-how with other businesses, providing opportunities for others to learn from their experience.
- They actively seek to improve and restore the health of individuals, communities and the planet
