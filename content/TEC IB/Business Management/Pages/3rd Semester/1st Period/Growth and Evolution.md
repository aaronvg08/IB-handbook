# Growth

Growth is a common business objective. For some businesses, growth means more market share, more profit, and a better reputation. However, expanding the business can also bring both risks and negative impacts in terms of environmental and social sustainability.

> [!important] Growing a business means expanding the business.

Growth can mean multiple things.

- **Growth in sales revenue**: Increasing the money earned from selling the product; this is calculated by multiplying the prices of products by the number of products sold.
- **Growth in profit**: Increasing the amount of money left over after costs of production have been subtracted from revenues.
- **Growth in market share**: Increasing the percentage of a given market represented by a business’s sales.
- **Growing impact**: Increasing the positive social and environmental consequences of the actions of the business.
- **Growing a resilient business ecosystem**: Generating opportunities for other businesses to grow and to strengthen their relationships with a wide range of stakeholders, distributing more of the value of the business to them.

## Internal

Internal growth is also referred to as organic growth. It refers to expansion that is carried out by the organization itself, without working with a partner.

| Advantages                                                          | Disadvantages                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Less expensive than external growth                                 | Much slower than external growth  |
| Less risky                                                          | Can create cash flow problems     |
| Maintain control                                                    | More limited than external growth |
| 100% enforces a company's values and [[Mission statement\|mission]] |                                   |

Taking the advantages and disadvantages in mind, here are some ways in which you can try to develop a company's internal growth:

- **Increasing production and gaining market share:** Businesses can increase the output of their current products if the market demands it. They can also increase their share of the existing market through pricing changes, improved promotion or improved distribution.
- **Developing new products:** Using market research, businesses can develop new products or improve their existing products to satisfy the target market and increase [[Revenue|revenue]].
- **Finding new markets:** Businesses can sell their existing products or services in a new or to a new group of people. Establishing new markets is one of the most cost-effective internal growth strategies, since it does not involve new product research, development and production.

## External

External growth methods often involve other organizations who offer to help yours, often in exchange for something else. 

| Advantages                                                        | Disadvantages                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Often faster than internal growth                                 | Riskier than internal growth, specially if large-scaled |
| Potential for [[Economies of scale\|economies of scale]]          | Can be hard to realize costs reductions                 |
| Can create synergies, increase employee talent pool and expertise | Possibility fo culture clash between organizations      |
| A competitor may be eliminated with the power of frienship        | Proprietary information and technology could be lost    |

- **[[Merger]]**: Two businesses agree to combine and become a single business.
- **[[Acquisition]]**: One company purchases another company with its permission.
- **[[Takeover]]**: One company purchases another company without its permission.
- **[[Joint venture]]**: A new enterprise is created by two companies.
- **[[Strategic alliance]]**: Two businesses collaborate to achieve a common goal.
- **[[Franchising]]**: A business sells the permission to run its business model to a person.

# Small businesses

Even though growth is a common business objective, some companies may decide to stay small and focus on satisfying their customers with excellent customer service and quality products.

>[!note]
>This also means that they are comfortable with the amount of profit they are making.

## Advantages

- **Avoiding risk, maintaining control:** Sometimes owners are not interested in growing because they do not want to take large risks or lose control over the business. They focus on achieving only the profits they need to survive and be comfortable. This is sometimes known as satisficing.
- **Small market size:** Even though increasing market share is one of the most common business objectives, there can be highly specialized professionals who only serve customers in the local area or a niche market and do not want to expand their services to other places or groups of people.
- **Limited access to sources of finance:** Often small businesses find it difficult to gain access to sources of finance in order to expand.
- **Sustainability:** Some small businesses may be concerned about environmental sustainability and they do not want to overuse resources.
- **Strong social networks:** Some businesses may decide to stay small in order to engage with other stakeholders, including customers and employees, in a positive way through strong networks.

## Disadvantages

- **High costs**: Small businesses tend to have higher costs of production because they cannot take advantage of [[Economies of scale|economies of scale]].
- **Low profits**: Not being able to expand as much can lead to lower profits.
- Access to finance: Accessing finances can be difficult, and some [[Sources of finance|external sources of finance]] may believe that it is too risky to invest in a smaller business.
- **Staff**: Small business often have trouble recruiting and retaining skilled workers, since larger businesses can offer higher salaries, greater benefits, and more professional development.
- **Competition**: Larger businesses have better prices, and if the company's customer service isn't that good, then most people will end up going to the competition instead.

# Generative/regenerative businesses

Some businesses work to grow and develop ecosystems in which they operate by being generous with those in their networks. They do not necessarily work to maximize profit, rather, they optimize profit and create opportunities of growth and development of the [[Stakeholders|stakeholders]] around them.

Some of the activities of generative/regenerative businesses include:

- Sharing their know-how with other businesses, providing opportunities for others to learn from their experience.
- Actively seeking to improve and restore the health of individuals, communities and the planet.
- Nurturing a network of relationships with their [[Stakeholders|stakeholders]] so that the interaction and interdependence support widespread thriving. This includes creating shared purpose and delivering value in many forms to their stakeholders, rather than just profit.

>[!example]
>Here are some examples of generative (regenerative) businesses:
>
>- A technology firm making its code open source so that others can learn from and improve it, or designing its platforms so that other businesses can connect with them.
>- A local bakery regularly offering free bread-baking classes for the community.
>- A multinational food manufacturer providing financial support for farmers to transition to [regenerative agriculture](https://regenerationinternational.org/why-regenerative-agriculture/).
>- A school offering empty classrooms for free (at weekends and during school holidays) to local non-profit organizations who need space to run their programs.
