# Market plan

A market plan is a document that outlines all the decisions a company will take to promote a product. It begins with identifying corporate business goals which, in turn, inform the marketing objectives. The business should conduct a marketing audit (a look at where the company is and where it wants to be) and establish marketing objectives based on the current position and goals.

The main elements of the marketing plan:

- **The marketing objective**: Will provide direction for the marketing function. If achieved, the marketing objective should help the company achieve its overall business objectives.
- **The marketing budget**: A budget is the amount of money that has been allocated to a task. 
- **[[Segmentation]] and the target market**: Market segmentation is breaking consumers into groups that have similar characteristics and needs. Once target markets have been identified, the business can develop marketing strategies to fulfill those needs.
- **Market research**: It involves gathering information about consumers’ needs, tastes, habits and preferences in order to aid marketing decisions. Effective market research allows companies to identify new opportunities, which can lead to increased sales.
- **Marketing strategies**: Setting up long-term actions in order to promote the business and/or product that it is selling. 
- **Control tools**: Tools that allow management to see whether or not strategies have been successful. It may include sales revenue, profit, statistics, etc.

# Product positioning

To compete against other brands within a market, companies need to differentiate themselves, positioning their brand to meet the needs of their selected target market. This limits potential competition and increases the chances of establishing customer loyalty.

>[!important]
>A differentiation of a business is also called a Unique Selling Point (USP)!

## Product positioning map

These kinds of maps are visual representations of how competitors position their brands in the eyes of the customers. A product positioning map usually displays two attributes, such as price and quality, or price and age. More complicated maps can include more than two attributes.

# Niche vs mass markets

When markets are characterized based on size, they are often divided into niche and mass markets. A niche market targets a small segment of the population, while a mass market refers to selling undifferentiated products to a large segment of a population.

- Niche market: Niche markets are very specialized markets where people have very specific wants that are different from the larger markets.
- Mass market: Mass markets are for goods that are produced and sold massively. They may even include entire populations.
