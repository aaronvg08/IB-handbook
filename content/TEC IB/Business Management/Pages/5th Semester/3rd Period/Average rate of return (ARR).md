The average rate of return (ARR) is an [[Investment appraisal|investment appraisal]] technique that expresses the annual forecasted returns as a percentage of the initial investment cost. Businesses will prefer investments with a higher ARR since it means they get their investment back quickly. Below is the formula for the ARR.

$$
\text{Average rate of return (ARR)}= \frac{(\text{Total returns}-\text{Capital cost})\div \text{Years of use}}{\text{Capital cost}}\times 100
$$

# Example

A manufacturing company is thinking about purchasing a new $200,000 machine. The steps to calculate the payback period.

Considering the following [[Cash flow|cash flow]] forecast. Here, we do not consider year 0, which would be the initial cost.

| Year | Cash inflow | Cash outlflow | Net cash flow |
| ---- | ----------- | ------------- | ------------- |
| 1    | $80,000     | $20,000       | $60,000       |
| 2    | $140,000    | $60,000       | $80,000       |
| 3    | $240,000    | $120,000      | $120,000      |
| 4    | $360,000    | $200,000      | $160,000      |

The total returns are $420,000 if you add all the cash flow. The capital cost, which is the amount that the machine costs, is $200,000. We use it for 4 years. So, we substitute everything in our equation.

$$
\text{Average rate of return (ARR)}=\frac{($420,000-$200,000)\div 4}{$200,000} \times 100
$$
$$
\text{Average rate of return (ARR)}=27.5\%
$$

This means that the company earns 27.5% of its investment every year.
