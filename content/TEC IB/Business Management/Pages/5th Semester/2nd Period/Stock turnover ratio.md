The stock turnover ratio is an [[Efficiency ratios|efficiency ratio]] that measures how often a business sells their assets and has to restock their supply. This can measured in both times per year and days, as seen below.

$$
\text{Stock turnover ratio (times)}=\frac{\text{Cost of sales}}{\text{Average stock}}
$$
$$
\text{Stock turnover ratio (days)}=\frac{\text{Cost of sales}}{\text{Average stock}}\times 365
$$

Likewise, average stock can be obtained like so:

$$
\text{Average stock}=\frac{\text{Opening stock} + \text{Closing stock}}{2}
$$

>[!warning]
>It is important to note that businesses in different industries have different stock turnover ratios, so when comparing these values, it's important for the businesses to be in the same industry.

There are some strategies to improve the stock turnover ratio if it's not great-looking:

- **Supply a narrower range of goods**: Simplifies the stock and increases control over it, but it may lead to a low-quality product.
- **Selling obsolete stock**: Reducing unpopular stock items can reduce stocks. However, limiting the product offering to customers may negatively affect revenue.
- **Selling high-demand stock**: Sell what's trending right now, which can improve sales, but it may give the wrong impression to customers who wanted something else.
- **Just-in-time stock**: Stock is ordered only when it is needed. However, there could be delays that can cause the business to run out of stock.
