The debtors days ratio is an [[Efficiency ratios|efficiency ratio]] that indicates the average number of days it takes a business to collect its debts. This metric is good to use in case the business sells goods or gives credit.

$$
\text{Debtor days}=\frac{\text{Creditors}}{\text{Sales revenue}}\times 365
$$

- A longer payment period than 30 days is worse for a business, since we want to collect the money we are owed as fast as possible.
- A shorter payment method than 30 days is better for a business since we can obtain our money faster.

>[!important]
>Remember that the creditors are obtained from the [[Statement of financial position|balance sheet]]. The cost of sales figure is usually found in the [[Statement of profit or loss|statement of profit or loss]].

There are some strategies a business can employ to improve its debtor days:

- **Have customers pay in cash**: The business could offer discounts for cash payments, charge interest for credit payments or only accept cash.
- **Shortening the credit period**: Shortening the credit period from 90 days to 60 or 30 days would mean debtors paying sooner.
- **Improving credit control**: The business could give trade credit only to customers with a record of paying on time.
- **Refuse bad credit**: Stopping deliveries to customers who have not yet paid for the product should also bring faster payment, reducing the debtor figures.
- **Threatening legal action**: The business could threaten the customer with legal action if they don't pay, which applies pressure on them to pay the business back.

>[!danger]
>All of these strategies are good, but they may give the wrong impression and/or upset the customer and lead to a decline in revenue.
