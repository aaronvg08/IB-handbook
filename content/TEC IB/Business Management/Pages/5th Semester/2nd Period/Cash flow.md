Cash flow has to do with the money moving in and out of the business. It's the real-time pulse of your financial operations.

- **Cash inflow**: Money received from customer payments, loans, or sales.
- **Cash outflow**: Money spent on rent, salaries, inventory, and operating expenses.

Net cash flow is defined as payments received by a business minus payments made. So, it means how much money you earned as a whole, disregarding taxes and credit and assets. The formula for cash flow is seen below.

$$
\text{Cash flow}=\text{Cash inflow}-\text{Cash outflow}
$$

>[!Question]
>Sometimes, if you sell something on credit, the profit is high and positive. However, cash flow is negative, since it is the money your business actually has, and no one paid you.

# Forecast

A cash flow forecast is done in order to try to predict how a business will financially perform in a specific period. A cash flow forecast includes the following:

- **Cash inflow**: The money flowing into the business.
- **Cash outflow**: The money flowing out of the business.
- **Net cash flow**: Difference between the total cash inflows and outflows.
- **Opening balance**: The amount of cash the business has at the beginning of the month.
- **Closing balance**: The amount of cash the business has at end of the month.

# Problems

Issues with cash flow usually mean that the company is unable to increase its inflows and/or control its outflows. At the end of the day, without cash, the business cannot pay its obligations and may become [[Insolvency|insolvent]].

Reasons for poor cash flow include:

- **Poor stock management**: The company has too much stock that they can't sell.
- **Poor pricing strategy**: Using an inadequate pricing strategy may decrease sales.
- **Extreme expansion**: If a company is investing in expanding too much to quickly, it can generate debts and lead to cash flow problems.
- **High [[Expenses|expenses]]**: If the business' expenses are too high, its outflow is higher than the inflows, creating trouble.
- **Seasonal demands**: If a product is more popular during a certain season, when that season passes, the company can experience losses.

>[!note]
>[[Non-profit social enterprise|Non-profit social enterprises]] also face cash flow problems since they rely so much on donations.

## Solutions

There are two things you can do to fix cash flow issues: increase the inflows, and decrease the outflows.

Some ways to increase the cash inflows of a company include:

- **Effective debt collection**: Ensure money is paid on time.
- **Cash transactions only**: Completely reject credit.
- **Increased promotion**: Implement more [[Marketing planning|marketing]] strategies to increase sales.
- **Expand product portfolio**: Create new products to diversify the business.
- **Going public**: Become a [[Publicly held company|publicly held company]] to sell shares.
- **Overdraft**: Take out [[Overdraft|overdrafts]] in order to increase cash flow through [[Debt capital|debt]].
- **Loans**: Borrowing money from the bank to increase cash flow through [[Debt capital|debt]].
- **Government assistance**: Get help or loans from the government.
- **[[Sale of assets]]**: Sell fixed assets that aren't needed.

Some ways to reduce and control the cash outflows include:

- **Better stock management**: Stock only goods that sell.
- **Renegotiate credit terms**: Get an extension in case your debt is due.
- **Switch to cheaper suppliers**: Get cheaper materials, though it affects product quality.
- **Reduce [[Expenses|expenses]]**: Cut unnecessary expenses, which could affect daily operations.
- **[[Leasing|Lease]]**: Rent equipment or land rather than buy it upfront.
