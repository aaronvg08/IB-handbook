The return on capital employed is a [[Profitability ratios|profitability ratio]] that measures how much of a company's assets are producing profit. It compares profit before interest and tax to capital employed. The formulas for both can be seen below.

$$
\text{ROCE}=\frac{\text{Profit before interest and tax}}{\text{Capital employed}}\times 100
$$
$$
\text{Capital employed}=\text{Non-current liabilities} + \text{Equity}
$$

>[!example]
>For instance, a ROCE of 44% means that for every $100 of resources (equity) used, the business generates $44 in profit.

>[!warning]
>To be used effectively, the ROCE has to be compared to another business in the same industry. The average ROCE in an industry is called the industry standard.

To improve the ROCE, there are some strategies you can use:

- **Increase revenue**: Special promotions, price changes, increasing distribution channels available to customers, and introducing new and improved products can all increase sales revenues.
- **Reduce cost of sales**: Using lower cost suppliers, improving stock control, and seeking [[Economies of scale|economies of scale]] can all improve the ROCE.
- **Sell unused assets**: Selling unused, obsolete and underused assets will improve the business’s operational efficiency and reduce costs.
- **Reduce long-term [[Liabilities|liabilities]]**: Paying off debt or negotiating lower interest rates or more attractive repayment terms on loans.
