The profit margin (PM) is a [[Profitability ratios|profitability ratio]] that shows the profit before income and tax as a percentage of sales [[Revenue|revenue]]. It's an important ratio since it shows how well managers control indirect costs. The formula can be seen below.

$$
\text{PM}=\frac{\text{Profit before interest and tax}}{\text{Sales revenue}}\times 100
$$

>[!example]
>A PM of 31% means that for every $100 in sales revenue the business generates $31 of profit after cost of sales and other expenses.

To improve the PM, a business can reduce the following expenses:

- Rent: The organization could negotiate a lower rental payment, or relocate to new premises that charge less. It could be inconvenient or have lower quality facilities, though.
- Electricity: You could monitor and lower the electricity bill by using alternative sources of energy or powering off devices. You need to ensure that this doesn't result in a worse product.
- Stationary: A business can try to cut down the use of paper by using technology. However, this also increases energy use.
- Administration costs: You can try to lower salaries or perks of employees, but this could potentially damage the employee's [[Motivation and demotivation|motivation]].
