The acid test ratio, also known as the quick ratio, is the same as the [[Current ratio|current ratio]] but excluding stock (inventory) from the equation, since they are the least liquid of current assets, and whether a business can sell its stock depends on a lot of things outside of the business' control.

You can calculate this with the following formula:

$$
\text{Acid test ratio}= \frac{\text{Current assets} - \text{Stock}}{\text{Current liabilities}}
$$

>[!example]
>An acid test ratio of 2.5 means that for every $1 of debt, the business has $2.5 in liquid assets to cover it.

>[!note]
>The strategies to improve the acid test ratio are usually the same as for the [[Current ratio|current ratio]].
