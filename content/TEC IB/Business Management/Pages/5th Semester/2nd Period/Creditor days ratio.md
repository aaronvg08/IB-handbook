The creditor days ratio is an [[Efficiency ratios|efficiency ratio]] that indicates the average number of days it takes a business to pay its suppliers and other creditors. This metric is crucial for understanding how well a business manages to pay its debt for their everyday cycle.

$$
\text{Debtor days}=\frac{\text{Creditors}}{\text{Sales revenue}}\times 365
$$

- A longer payment period is better for cash flow. It preserves working capital and reduces pressure on the cash conversion cycle.
- A shorter payment period may strain available cash for operations and limit flexibility in managing daily expenses.

>[!important]
>Remember that the creditors are obtained from the [[Statement of financial position|balance sheet]]. Cost of sales is usually found in the [[Statement of profit or loss|statement of profit or loss]].

There are some ways to improve the creditor days ratio:

- **Negotiating longer credit periods**: Businesses can develop close relationships with their suppliers and negotiate, but asking for these in the first place can threaten the relationship itself if there isn't enough trust.
- **Just-in-time stock**: Stock is ordered just when it is needed. However, this could lead to delays that then cause the business to run out of stock.
- **Look for other suppliers**: Working with different suppliers might help businesses get better credit terms and might extend credit periods. However, they need to stablish a relationship with a new supplier again.
