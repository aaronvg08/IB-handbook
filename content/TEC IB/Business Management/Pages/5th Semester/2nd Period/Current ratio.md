The current ratio is a [[Liquidity ratios|liquidity ratio]] that calculates how well the business' [[Assets|assets]] can cover and pay its short-term [[Liabilities|liabilities]]. The formula for the current ratio can be seen below.

$$
\text{Current ratio}=\frac{\text{Current assets}}{\text{Current liabilities}}
$$

>[!example]
>A current ratio of 3.5 means that for every $1 a business owns, they have $3.5 in liquid current assets to pay those liabilities.

There are some strategies to improve the current ratio if it's not doing well. These are some ways to increase your current assets.

- **Increase sales**: If you increase the money you earn, now you have more money to pay your debts. However, this is vague and it can be tricky to know whether to increase or decrease prices.
- **Reduce debtor's figure**s: Asking customers who buy on credit to pay sooner with cash can increase cash assets. It may cause the business to lose customers though.
- **Sell unused fixed [[Assets|assets]]**: The sale of unused assets such as old trucks and machinery could help bring in more cash. You can't sell assets that you really need though.
- **Reduce drawings**: Drawings are cash withdrawals from the business for the owner's personal use. Stop doing that. Though sometimes it's not possible if the owners themselves don't have a salary.

Now, here are some ways to decrease your liabilities:

- **Extend credit period**: Ask for an extension on the payment. Though asking for this can threaten the relationship and trust between the creditor and debtor.
- **Decrease overheads**: Overheads such as rent, administrative staff salaries and stationery constitute a cash outflow. However, if you do this, you may cause [[Motivation and demotivation|demotivation]] to employees if you start selling and downgrading everything.
- **Reduce current liabilities**: Pay your debts idiot. This only works if you have enough money to do so, though.
