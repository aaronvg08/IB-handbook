Depreciation happens when an [[Assets|asset]] starts to lose value or disappears completely. Depreciation can sometimes be a [[Cost|cost]] or an [[Expenses|expense]] depending on what is being depreciating. 

- **Cost**: When something relating to a product's manufacturing process becomes depreciated.
- **Expense**: When anything else becomes depreciated.

Depreciation can also happen in two ways when it comes to fixed assets:

- **Wear & tear**: When a machine stops working completely or stops being as efficient as normal because of how old it is.
- **Obsolescence**: When the technology used in the asset has been surpassed by more recent innovations. Over time, machinery will be replaced by new and more efficient machines.

# Calculating

There are different methods for calculating how an asset depreciates over time. There are multiple ways of doing:

## Straight-line method

The straight-line method of depreciation is used to calculate the fall in value of an asset evenly over its useful life. This method uses the following formula.

$$
\text{Annual depreciation}=\frac{\text{adquisition cost}-\text{residual value}}{\text{estimated useful life}}
$$

>[!note]
>Residual value is what you can get after the product stops working or needs to be replaced. So, if you [[Sale of assets|sell the asset]], you can get some money back.

### Example

Chinon Africa Investments Ltd. purchases a delivery truck to transport fresh fruits and vegetables. This truck costed $100,000. It has an estimated residual value of $25,000 and a useful life of 5 years.

We use our formula.

$$
\frac{$100,000-$25,000}{5}=$15,000
$$

Therefore, we know that every year this asset looses $15,000. We can show this in a chart to show the depreciation over the years.

| Year | Depreciation   | Value    |
| ---- | -------------- | -------- |
| 0    | 0$ (brand new) | $100,000 |
| 1    | -$15,000       | $85,000  |
| 2    | -$15,000       | $70,000  |
| 3    | -$15,000       | $55,000  |
| 4    | -$15,000       | $40,000  |
| 5    | -$15,000       | $25,000  |

>[!tip]
>In order to check if you did it correctly, then your residual value should be the same as what you get in the end.

## Units of production method

The units of production method of depreciation is a method of calculating the loss in value of an asset by estimating the units produced annually. 

To determine the annual depreciation, the business must create a production plan. This plan will outline the estimated production output per year during the time period that the asset will be used.

$$
\text{Annual depreciation}=\frac{\text{yearly units of production}}{\text{total estimated lifetime production}}\times \text{original value of asset}
$$

### Example

Company A acquires machinery on January 1, 2022, for $20,000. It is expected to produce 8,000 units over 4 years with a residual value of $0.

We have to run our formula for each year, since each year we are producing a different amount.

| Year      | Units produced | Depreciation                                                 | Value   |
| --------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| 1         | 1,000          | $\left( \frac{1,000}{\$8,000} \right)\cdot \$20,000=\$2,500$ | $17,500 |
| 2         | 2,000          | $\left( \frac{2,000}{\$8,000} \right)\cdot \$20,000=\$5,000$ | $12,500 |
| 3         | 2,000          | $\left( \frac{2,000}{\$8,000} \right)\cdot \$20,000=\$5,000$ | $7,500  |
| 4         | 3,000          | $\left( \frac{3,000}{\$8,000} \right)\cdot \$20,000=\$7,500$ | $0      |
| **Total** | 8,000          | -$20,000                                                     |         |

>[!tip]
>In order to check if you did it correctly, then your residual value should be the same as what you get in the end.
