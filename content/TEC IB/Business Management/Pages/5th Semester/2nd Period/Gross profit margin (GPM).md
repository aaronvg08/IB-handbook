The gross profit margin (GPM) is a [[Profitability ratios|profitability ratio]] that compares the [[Gross profit|gross profit]] to the sales revenue. It is calculated with the following formula and expressed as a percentage.

$$
\text{GPM}=\frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Sales revenue}}\times 100
$$

>[!example]
>A GPM of 55% means that for every $100 of revenue the business generates a profit of $55.

To improve the GPM there are multiple strategies:

- **Diversification**: Different products may have higher GPMs than others. However, this can be costly and risky.
- **Lowering prices**: Making the product's price lower can increase sales. However, this only works if the business achieves [[Economies of scale|economies of scale]] to lower costs of production.
- **Increasing prices**: Increasing the product's price can also help increase revenue, especially where the product has little competition or loyal customers. It can't really work in a competitive market.
- **[[Economies of scale]]**: Purchasing economies of scale would reduce the unit cost. However, this is only really available to large companies.
- **Low-cost suppliers**: Suppliers that sell for lower can decrease cost of sales, but it can also threaten the quality of the products you sell.
- **Reducing direct labor costs**: Productivity can be improved and costs can be reduced by hiring fewer workers. However, pressure on product workers could increase [[Labor turnover|labor turnover]] and increase costs of [[Recruitment|recruitment]] and [[Training|training]].
