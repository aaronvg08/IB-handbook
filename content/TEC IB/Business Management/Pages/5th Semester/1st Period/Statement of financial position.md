The statement of financial positions, commonly called a balance sheet, serves as a financial snapshot frozen in time. Think of it as a detailed photograph of everything your business owns and owes at a specific moment.

It has three core components:

- [[Assets]]: Everything the business owns that has value.
- [[Liabilities|Liabilities]]: All debts and financial obligations of the business.
- [[Equity]]: The owners' stake in the business.

> [!info] Profit
> In Equity, you also add [[Profit|profit]] since it can affect the overall value of the company.

# Benefits

The statement of financial position is more than just numbers on a page. It's a powerful tool that drives critical business decisions and reveals the true financial story of any organization.

- **For managers and investors:**
	- Financial health assessment
	- Liquidity analysis
	- Capital structure insights.
- **For strategic decision making:**
	- Investment opportunities
	- Business expansion and growth planning.
	- Risk management and financial planning.

# How-to

## Step 1: Understanding assets

[[Assets]] represent all resources of value that a business owns and controls. These valuable resources generate future economic benefits and form the foundation of business operations.

![[Assets#Current vs non-current]]

![[financial pos assets.png|center|450]]

## Step 2: Understanding liabilities

[[Liabilities]] represent all debts, obligations, and amounts owed by the business to external parties. These financial obligations must be settled through the transfer of assets, provision of services, or other economic benefits.

![[Liabilities#Current vs non-current]]

![[financial pos liabilities.png|center|400]]
## Step 3: Net assets and equity

Net assets represent the true value owned by shareholders after all liabilities are subtracted from total assets. This figure must equal the total equity shown on the balance sheet.

![[financial pos final.png|center|400]]

>[!success]
>As you can see, the net assets and total equity are the same. This is what we mean by balance sheet, they **must** be equal, otherwise, you made a mistake.

>[!note]
>For a [[Non-profit social enterprise|non-profit]] enterprise, it is the same process, except that there are no shareholders, so there is no share capital in the equity.
