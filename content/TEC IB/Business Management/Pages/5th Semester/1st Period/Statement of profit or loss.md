The statement of profit or loss of a company, also known as an income statement, shows the profit or loss that is generated by a business from its trading activities. The statement of profit or loss is constructed differently depending on whether the business is a for-profit enterprise or a non-profit enterprise.

![[profit & loss final.png|center|400]]
# For-profit enterprise

The statement of profit and loss changes for whether or not your company is [[For-profit commercial enterprise|for-profit]] or not. This entire section is an example. 

## Step 1: Get gross profit (Green)

You need to calculate the gross profit first, and state both costs and revenues from it.

![[Gross profit]]

![[profit & loss step 1.png|center|450]]

## Step 2: Calculate expenses (Yellow)

Subtract indirect costs ([[Expenses|expenses]]) from your gross profit. These are costs not directly tied to production, such as rent, administration costs and other expenses that are not directly attributable to the production of the product. This way, we calculate [[Profit|profit]]. 

![[profit & loss step 2.png|center|450]]

## Step 3: List how profit is distributed (orange)

The third section of the statement of profit or loss shows how the profits are distributed. There are two items in this section:

- **Dividends**: Money paid to shareholders from the profit after interest and tax. It is used as a treat for the shareholders to reward them for being there.
- **Retained profit**: Money that the company keeps, after everything. This is what's defines whether or not the statement is of profit or loss

![[profit & loss final.png|center|450]]

# Non-profit

When your company is a non-profit social enterprise, the process for making the statement is a bit different.

## Step 1: Get gross surplus (Green)

You need to get the [[Gross profit|gross surplus]] first, and subtract it from the expenses and interest.

![[Gross profit]]

![[profit & loss (non-profit) step 1.png|center|450]]

## Step 2: Calculate expenses (Yellow)

Subtract indirect costs ([[Expenses|expenses]]) from your gross profit. These are costs not directly tied to production, such as rent, administration costs and other expenses that are not directly attributable to the production of the product. This way, we calculate [[Profit|retained surplus]].

![[profit & loss (non-profit) final.png|center|450]]

And that's it! There are no dividends for non-profit social enterprises, so you are done.
