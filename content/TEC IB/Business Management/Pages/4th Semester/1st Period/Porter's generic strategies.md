Porter's generic strategies is a business toolkit of four general strategies that allows a business to create a [[Marketing planning|market plan]] considering its external circumstances and target market. It's also used to analyze a business and how its marketing strategy is doing.

![[porter's generic strategies.png|center|500]]

The strategies are the following:

- **Cost leadership**: Having a low cost in a large market. This is usually achieved through [[Economies of scale|economies of scale]], specialized technologies or more efficient supply chains.
- **Differentiation**: This is when a business is able to make its product better or different from competitors in the industry, where the industry is very large. This is usually done through a Unique Selling Point (USP).
- **Cost focus**: This is when a business has a low cost product in a niche market. A business that is pursuing this strategy will usually be producing a product that is relatively basic, perhaps a lower-cost copy of a popular and higher-priced product.
- **Differentiation focus**: This means that the business is producing a specialized, or differentiated, product for a niche market. It could be different due to the quality or inherent characteristics of the product.
- **Stuck in the middle**: A business is stuck in the middle when it is not differentiated enough to convince consumers to buy its product. The product may not be cheap enough either, and they could be aiming for too many or an unclear market.
