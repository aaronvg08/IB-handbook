Communication is the exchange of information between people, organizations, or between different organizations. Bad communication can hinder the ability of businesses to perform better, while good communication helps a business run smoothly.

>[!danger]
>Complex language used in communication puts up barriers between employees and managers and implicitly sends messages about power and authority.

# Formal

Formal communication is a type of communication that happens over official channels. It flows through an organization’s developed hierarchical chain of command. Employees have to follow these defined communication channels to perform their duties.

| Advantages                                                                                            | Disadvantages                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Increases efficiency, coordination and accuracy. Ensures that the information reaches its target.     | Is too slow, and can sometimes delay decision-making in critical situations.                        |
| Increases discipline, order, and has fewer mistakes. Leads to orderly communication without missteps. | Employees may feel like they can't communicate as well and it may reduce iniciative and creativity. |
| Documented for future reference, and avoids miscommunication.                                         | It is expensive, specially when it comes to digital transfers of information.                       |

## Types

There are some specific types of formal communication:

- **Vertical communication**: Communication takes place between different organizational levels.
- **Horizontal communication**: Communication takes place between two employees working in different departments of an organization, but on the same hierarchical level.
- **Diagonal communication**: Communication takes place between employees working at different hierarchical levels and in different departments.

## Spoken

- **Telephone communication**: This is for communicating simple messages and receiving immediate feedback. However, there is often no written record of the conversation.
- **Meetings**: These could be board meetings, shareholder meetings or meetings between different departments or within a department. Meetings are usually recorded and allow for interactions between the stakeholders involved, like asking questions.
- **Presentations**: These allow for communication of new ideas, projects or requirements of starting something new. Detailed information is passed on and a record of the communication is maintained and sent to the attendees.
- **Interviews**: This method involves a small number of people and is used to recruit new staff. It is detailed and allows two-way communication.

## Written

- **Business letters**: Letters sent through the mail. These might include letters to government offices or letters arranging meetings with officials.
- **Reports**: Weekly or monthly reports within the business may provide information about sales targets achieved or low sales. Financial performances of businesses are also communicated through reports.

## Electronic

- **Email**: The same as business letters, except digital and can be sent and received immediately. This has become one of the most common methods of communication.
- **Websites**. These are used to communicate mainly with external stakeholders. A good website shows a positive view of the business and makes it look good.
- **Social media**. Businesses advertise through Facebook, Instagram, Twitter and other platforms. Products are promoted, advertised and sold through social media websites. A business’s contact details can also be gathered through these networks.
- **Text messages**. Businesses send text messages to new and existing customers informing them about launches of new products or services. Texting is an efficient method of communication.
- **Intranets**. An intranet is a private network used for the sharing of information and internal communication within a business. A business can have offices in many locations and an intranet enables the flow of information.
- **Video conferencing**. Face-to-face interviews and meetings with internal and external stakeholders can be held over video. Zoom, Slack and Microsoft Teams meetings have become popular platforms for video conferencing.

# Informal

Informal communication flows freely in an organization, without defined channels. It happens between any employee of the organization and is way faster than formal communication. 

| Advantages                                                                           | Disadvantages                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Faster and absolutely free since it doesn't use official channels.                   | Information isn't confidencial and can be shared outside of the desired group. |
| Employees that share information through casual talk have better relationships.      | Information can be distorted and can lead to misinformation.                   |
| Some information cannot be sent through formal channels, so it's a good alternative. | Alternative types of information may lead to rumors and false allegations.     |

## Single-strand chain

This happens when an employee says something to another employee, and then that employee says it to another, forming a chain.

![[single-strand chain.png|center|400]]

>[!example]
>This is like playing broken phone.

## Cluster chain

This happens when a group of people gather and talk about something that is usually of mutual interest. The information is spread, as the name states, over clusters.

![[cluster chain.png|center|200]]

>[!warning]
>This is the slowest form of informal communication.

## Probability chain

An employee transfers information to a random employee, and that employee transfers information to another random person, and so on.

![[probability chain.png|center|300]]

>[!note]
>This usually happens when people speak of something to another in casual conversation.

## Gossip chain

An employee says something to a group of employees, and those employees spread the information to another group. 

![[gossip chain.png|center|200]]

>[!success]
>This is the fastest form of informal communication

# Barriers to communication

Sometimes, the information that a person needs doesn't reach them in time. Broken or ineffective communication in a business can lead to [[Motivation and demotivation|demotivation]] and in some cases, conflict.

Here are some common barriers that slow down or stop communication:

- **Cultural barriers**: If the [[Organizational culture|culture]] varies from department to department, it can lead to a [[Cultural clash|cultural clash]] and misinformation as a result.
- **Linguistic barriers**: If the people communicating don't share a common language, it can lead to difficulties communicating. This is specially true for [[Multinational companies (MNCs)|multinational companies]].
- **Emotional barriers**: Being too stressed, angry, or depressed can cause issues when communicating information and when receiving information.
- **Psychological and physiological barriers**: Fear of public speaking, speech disorders, etc. may make it more difficult for the sender to convey information.
- **Physical barriers**: Sometimes there are physical barriers, like a noisy environment or the inability to contact specific people because they are somewhere else.
- **Organizational barriers**: Some businesses have an excess of [[Bureaucracy|bureaucracy]] and structure, which can delay the process of sending information.
- **Attitude and personality barriers**: Differences in personality may lead to misinterpretation of communication.
- **Perception barriers**: People in organizations may view or perceive things differently. This should be taken into consideration when making decisions.
- **Technological barriers**: If an application or technological system fails, it can delay or even make communication impossible. Try not to rely too much on technology because of this.
- **Communication skills**: A message that lacks clarity may confuse the receiver. Likewise, some people don't pay attention and can misinterpret a message.
- **Form of communication**: If the inappropriate type of communication is chosen, it can make it so that not everyone sees the message or it doesn't reach its intended target.

## Overcoming barriers

### Direct strategies

- **Using simple and clear language**: Written and spoken communication should use clear language so that everyone understands the message. 
- **Active listening**: Employees at every level should be active listeners. Asking questions and repeating what the communicator has said to confirm understanding are two important ways to listen actively.
- **Managing emotional states**: When people feel overwhelmed, they may communicate frustration or anger along with the information they wish to convey. This can cause misunderstandings and even conflict, so managing emotional states at work is important for communication. 
- **Increasing contact**: Managers and employees who spend more time together may be able to better judge when and how to use formal and informal communication appropriately. Establishing constructive relationships opens the channels of communication.
### Indirect strategies

- **Recruiting for communication skills**: Human resource managers should include good communication skills in the person specification for open positions in the organization.
- **Induction and other [[Training|training]]**: When an employee joins a business, induction training could help them understand the expectations around formal and informal communication in the organization.
- **Simplifying organizational structure**: When organisations get too ‘tall’ the many levels of hierarchy and long chains of command can impede communication. When this occurs, businesses may consider [[Delayering|delayering]] to simplify organizational structure.
- **Changing appraisal methods**: A move to more appropriate [[Appraisal|appraisal]] methods can improve feedback in the organization, resulting in more consistent and effective communication between employees.
