Employees should feel happy about working in a company. However, this is not always the case. Conflicts between employees and employers in the workplace occur in most countries and can result in industrial disputes.

>[!note]
>Industrial disputes are disagreements between an employer and its employees.

# Sources of conflict

- **Values**: Different values and beliefs about what is important or moral may cause disagreement across employees and management.
- **[[Communication]]**: Positive communication makes a workplace prosper. If the communication in an organization makes employees feel like they aren't valued, it leads to conflict.
- **Power**: If a manager or the organization as a whole abuses its power onto the employees, workers may feel undermined by the system and feel like their voices aren't heard.
- **Insufficient resources**: You may not always have enough money to pay an employee. The manager’s decision may clash with the employee’s own perception of what they deserve.
- **Perceptions**: People have different ways of interpreting decisions and events. As a result, when people see a situation quite differently, conflicts can occur.
- **Change**: Changes in the business, whether it is due to inside or outside circumstances, can be stressful and lead to conflict.
- **Performance**: If people aren't good at their job, it will cause trouble.
- **Inequity**: If workers feel that they are not given a fair chance to secure promotions, take on extra responsibility or receive fair pay, then tensions can result.
- **Redundancies**: Businesses have to fire people sometimes. If this happens and workers don't know why, it can create tension and hostility.
- **Toxic workplace culture**: Some businesses discriminate against some people or engage in bullying, which may be a part of its [[Organizational culture|organizational culture]], and always causes conflict.

# Collective bargaining

One of the ways a business can avoid industrial action is through collective bargaining. This is when the company and employees, usually represented through a trade union, sit down and discuss what is happening.

It benefits employees since they feel represented in a group and feel like their needs are heart. It is also good for the company since they only have to negotiate with a group instead of with many individuals. Once an agreement is reached, all employees follow it, too.

# Avoiding conflict

To avoid industrial action from ever taking place, you can use the following methods:

- **Employee participation and industrial democracy**: These involve workers making decisions and sharing responsibility and authority in the workplace. Workers can participate in running the business and having a say in it.
- **No-strike agreements**: Many unions include a no-strike clause in their contracts to combat negative publicity from stoppages. This means precisely what it says: workers will not strike. They are usually made once an agreement has been made with management.
- **Single union agreements**: These are agreements whereby the employer will only negotiate with a single recognized labour union. This minimizes disruption and conflict that might be caused by dealing with multiple unions, as different unions may have different objectives.

Here are some strengths and weaknesses with these methods:

| Method                                              | Strengths                                                                                                           | Weaknesses                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Employee participation and industrial democracy** | Not only does this avoid conflict, but it can also [[Motivation and demotivation\|motivate]] employees as a result. | Democratic systems may slow down decision-making in the long run.                                          |
| **No-strike agreements**                            | This reduces the risk of disruption caused by strike action.                                                        | This may and usually does limit the bargaining power of the trade union.                                   |
| **Single union agreements**                         | This avoids wasting time by dealing with a bunch of unions that the organization may not recognize.                 | Some workers may feel the union that is doing the negotiating is ineffective in defending their interests. |
# Resolving conflict

Once conflict has started, it can be difficult to de-escalate. Most times, it's not useful for the business to attempt to escalate the conflict and try to 'win'. Therefore, here are some ways to resolve the conflict in a peaceful way.

- Conciliation: A neutral third-party listens to the demands of both sides and helps them negotiate an agreement which both parties are happy with.
- Arbitration: A neutral third-party listens to the demands of both sides and then decides what happens. The decision is legally-binding and unappealable.

Here are some strengths and weaknesses on both methods.

| Method           | Strengths                                                                                                                                        | Weaknesses                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Conciliation** | The privacy of both sides is preserved, so the business keeps their reputation and the conciliator is usually an expert.                         | There is no legally binding decision, and there is no guarantee that the conciliation will actually resolve anything.                                        |
| **Arbitration**  | It is much cheaper than a court battle and there is much more control over the conflict resolution process. The arbitrator is usually an expert. | The decision that the arbitrator makes is legally binding, and both sides have to obligue or face legal consequences. The decision can't be appealed either. |
