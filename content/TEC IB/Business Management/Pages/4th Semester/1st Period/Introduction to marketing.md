Marketing is the process in which a business identifies the needs that customers have in order to satisfy them. Marketing strategies will help a business meet its overall [[Business objectives|business objectives]].

The marketing mix is all elements that make up a marketing strategy. In order for a marketing strategy to be successful, it has to contain all the elements in a coherent way that makes sense for the product.

# Market

A market can mean different things, depending on what you're talking about:

- **A place where buyers and sellers come together or interact**: Like an actual physical marketplace. It's a place, physical or virtual, where buyers and sellers come together to exchange goods.
- **A location**: A specific geographical place like a country. This is specially relevant to [[Multinational companies (MNCs)|multinational companies]].
- **A type of product**: A market also refers to a specific types of product, for example the mobile phone market or the recycled paper market.


# Product orientation

Companies that prioritize the quality of their products over the needs of the market are called product-oriented companies. Product-oriented companies are inward facing. They have faith in their designers and engineers to come up with new and innovative ideas that can be passed on to the marketing department to sell.

| Advantages                                                                                                                            | Disadvantages                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The products of the company are highly specialized and can offer a unique selling point (USP).                                        | There is a high risk when developing products since there is no guarantee that people will actually buy it.                               |
| New products may receive a patent. This would enable the business to be the sole producer for a period of time and create a monopoly. | Product orientation requires large sums for investment. That money that could be used elsewhere in the business on less risky activities. |
| Developing new products that haven't been made before means there can be little or no competition to the company.                     |                                                                                                                                           |

# Market orientation

When companies are market-oriented, that means that they prioritize the needs of the market and what the customer wants above anything else. Extensive and ongoing market research will be at the centre of all decision making. New products are only developed when the business fully understands the needs and wants of the customers.

| Advantages                                                                                                                                             | Disadvantages                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| There is a lower risk in being market-oriented since you know people are more likely to buy your product since they need it.                           | Other companies can also be market-oriented and do the same, so you cannot develop a unique selling point (USP).                |
| With high product-market fit, repeat business is more likely, resulting in increased revenues and profits.                                             | Market research must be thorough and right. Poor market research results in poor product development and low sales and revenue. |
| [[Social enterprise\|Social enterprises]] are usually market oriented in order to meet human needs or to solve problems in society or the environment. | Businesses must be responsive to changing market conditions, which can be difficult for some companies                          |

# Market leadership

The market leader is the product or brand with the highest [[Market share|market share]]. Being the market leader is almost always a good thing. However, when one company dominates a market, there can be disadvantages for other stakeholders.

## Advantages for a business

- **Distribution channels**: A business who is the market leader will usually be on multiple distribution channels. Retailers will stock the brands that their customers demand.
- **Brand recognition**: People are more likely to recognize the brand and products of the market leader. The brand recognition also leads to more sales.
- **[[Economies of scale]]**: A business who is the market leader is the biggest, and can therefore benefit from multiple economies of scale.
- **Price leadership**: The price that the market leaders set will seem like the ‘right’ price for the product category. A market leader can purposely cause trouble for other companies through setting a higher or different price.

>[!note]
>There are also disadvantages for the business such as [[Diseconomies of scale|diseconomies of scale]] and disadvantages for the environment and the local economy. It's not always great to be great.

## Advantages & disadvantages for customers

| Issue      | Advantages                                                                                                        | Disadvantages                                                                                                                    |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| Price      | The business achieves [[Economies of scale\|economies of scale]], which can result in lower prices for customers. | A large company may not want to lower their prices, even if they do achieve economies of scale. They might raise prices instead. |
| Innovation | The business may achieve high profits, which can be reinvested into the business and breed innovation.            | If the business dominates the market, it may have little competition. It may not have the incentive to innovate. (cough, apple)  |
