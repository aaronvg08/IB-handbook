Sales forecasting is a strategy in [[Introduction to marketing|marketing]] where a business uses tools and data to predict the levels of sales that will happen in the future. These sales may be forecast either in quantity of products, or in total revenue earned.

| Uses                                                                                                                                    | Limitations                                                                                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Being based on past data can add validity to the results, assuming that past trends continue.                                           | New companies don't have enough data to make any type of historical analysis.                                                           |
| If correct, forecasts can help companies to plan for the future. They can hire or make staff redundant, among other actions             | Rapidly changing markets can lead forecasts to be invalid, since it assumes that past trends continue, and this may not always be true. |
| If a drop in sales is forecast, companies can react to this with increased marketing budgets in an effort to increase sales.            | Forecasts should only be seen as a guide. Managers need to remain flexible rather than following plans blindly.                         |
| If forecasts are extremely negative, a company may decide to withdraw a product from the market before it becomes a drain on resources. | Quantitative analysis isn't everything. Correlation doesn't equal causation, and to assume that can be dangerous for a business.        |

>[!important]
>By forecasting sales, a business can take decisions to ensure the max profitability of future sales, or take steps to stop an incoming disaster.

# Casual models

A casual model is a method where a business tries to find a casual relationship between two variables, an independent variable such as product pricing or production, and a dependent variable, which is usually sales. This allows the business to understand the relationship and any possible correlation between the two variables.

# Time series analysis and moving averages

Time series analysis is a method where a business analyses historical data, such as sales and revenue figures, and assumes that trends will continue.

However, there are very often significant variations in time series data. When this occurs, the data needs to be smoothed out by plotting moving average. The smoothed data is then given a line of best fit, which can be extrapolated to make a sales forecast.

## Seasonal variations

Seasonal variations in data occur when products have higher sales volumes at certain times of the year. Some products may experience a peak in the summer months, such as sunscreen, certain clothes and holidays. 

>[!example]
>Children’s toys sales peak at major gift-giving holidays in particular countries. 

>[!success]
>Understanding and calculating seasonal variations helps businesses to improve their sales forecasts.

## Cyclical variations

Cyclical variations in sales data occur when sales are affected by the economic cycle and how well a business is doing. This can be a cycle.

- Sales of certain goods, such as cars and televisions, increase when the economy is growing and employment is high. 
- Sales of the same items may decline during recessions, when incomes decline and unemployment increases.

## Random variations

Random variations are marked changes to sales data caused by unpredictable events. Such events could include a natural disaster, a major sporting event or political unrest. Such significant events can affect sales of various products in unpredictable ways.

# Qualitative analysis

Businesses cannot rely only on past quantitative data to make sales forecasts and decisions about their marketing mix. Businesses must also ensure that they understand broad trends in the external environment that might affect product sales, such as preferences and behaviors of consumers. Thus, businesses should also use qualitative analysis, such as [[Market research|market research]].

>[!example]
>A business that relied only on quantitative data from past sales to make decisions during the COVID-19 pandemic, for example, would have failed very quickly.
