The marketing mix are all the elements that make up a [[Marketing planning|marketing strategy]]. They refer to everything that happens between the customers and the business, and how it can be sharpened to create a Unique Selling Point (USP).

# The 7 Ps

The marketing mix is usually thought up as seven Ps where the first four elements refer to the marketing of goods, and all seven elements refer to service markets. Because most products involve both goods and services, many businesses use all seven elements of the marketing mix to plan their marketing strategy.

## Product

A product is a tangible result of a production process where resources are used to create a good or service to satisfy a need or a want. Businesses must ensure that products match customer needs and expectations.

### Life-cycle

The product life-cycle is a model that helps businesses make decisions about a product’s marketing mix. The model shows a product’s typical trend in sales over time. As products move through the stages, a business may want to change the marketing mix to achieve different objectives. The product life cycle is split into five stages:

- Research and development (R&D): The business investigates, creates, and prepares the production of the product they are selling based on the [[Market research|market research]] conducted.
- Introduction: The product is released to the market Consumers can buy the product if they wish. Initial sales are likely to be low, but they will be growing.
- Growth: The increases in product sales accelerate. The percentage change in sales is increasing.
- Maturity: A time of high and steady sales revenue. Sales will still be growing, but perhaps at a slower and slower rate. The market may be crowded, or saturated with competition.
- Decline: The product stops being sold because maybe it's Unique Selling Point has passed, it has been outdated by newer technologies, or people simply don't want it anymore.

There are some ways that a business can extend its product life-cycle:

| Method                      | Description                                                                    | Advantage                                                                                     | Disadvantage                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Finding a new target market** | If the product no longer works for the current market, it may work for others. | Sales can increase if the product is well-received by the new market.                         | You need to conduct more [[Market research\|market research]].                                               |
| **Redesigning of packaging**    | Changing the materials of the packaging, how it looks, how it's sold.          | Can make the product feel fresh and new, increasing sales and demand.                         | Some customers may feel tricked or disappointed if you're selling the same product with different packaging. |
| **Product update**              | Adding new features or upgrading the product.                                  | Updates can mostly be done at a low cost.                                                     | Some loyal customers may not like the new change.                                                            |
| **Price reduction**             | Lowering the price of the product                                              | Allows more customers to buy the product and prevents them from switching to the competition. | The price reduction can harm the company's reputation and can be perceived of lower quality.                 |
| **More promotion**              | Using promotional campaigns to encourage people to buy the product.            | Simple and easy to do, which can increase sales.                                              | Customers may just be tired of the product and this doesn't always work.                                     |

### Branding

A brand is a name, symbol, or idea that is connected to a company. A strong brand can give a product a character with which consumers can identify.

- **Brand awareness**: When people recognize the brand from its style or symbol.
- **Brand development**: The process of creating a strong brand.
- **Brand loyalty**: When customers prefer a brand over others.
- **Brand value**: The intellectual value of the brand, which is called a [[Trademark|trademark]], or when customers add additional value to a product because of the brand.

### Packaging

Customers do judge a book by its cover. A good packaging can be eye-catching and communicate what the product does and why it's so good. Most customers decide whether or not to buy a product based on the packaging.

Packaging covers the following roles:

- Protects the product, especially if it is fragile or a liquid.
- Communicates information, like quality or benefits of using.
- Promotes the product's unique selling point.
- Makes the product easy to use.

## Price

The amount of money that a business charges for its product. Businesses must choose the right price for their products based on [[Business objectives|business objectives]], costs of production, consumer expectations and level of competition.

### Pricing methods

Prices are hard to put. If the price is set too high, customers may not be willing to pay it. The business will lose sales. If the price is set too low, the revenues may not be high enough to cover costs of production.

Here are some ways to assign prices. Use wisely!

- **Cost-plus (markup) pricing**: All direct costs are considered, and then a percentage or markup is added to allow for profit.
- **Penetration pricing**: The business sets a low initial price to attract customers. Once loyalty is stablished, the business switches to another method.
- **Loss leader pricing**: Selling a product lower than its production costs to advertise the company instead of the product. Other products of the business are promoted, while the product generates losses.
- **Predatory pricing**: A company sets a low price due to their [[Economies of scale|economies of scale]] or size and forces competition out of the market, stablishing a monopoly. This is illegal in many countries.
- **Premium pricing**: A product's price is set very high, even if the production costs aren't that high, to keep customers thinking that the quality of the product is amazing.
- **Dynamic pricing (HL)**: Sell the product at different prices over time depending on whether the company things people are willing to pay more or if demand is high.
- **Competitive pricing (HL)**: Setting the price at the same level as its competitors.
- **Contribution pricing (HL)**: Only production costs are considered, and then money is added on top of that value to make the price. That extra money is called contribution.

| Method                         | Benefits                                                 | Limitations                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cost-plus (markup) pricing** | Simple and ensures all costs are covered.                | Takes no account of the market.                                                                   |
| **Penetration pricing**        | Customer loyalty and market share is stablished quickly. | Low profits are likely during initial sales and customers may not accept the sudden price change. |
| **Loss leader pricing**        | Can lead to a large boost in revenue.                    | It's only product for multi-product retailers.                                                    |
| **Predatory pricing**          | Competition is wiped out.                                | It's illegal in many countries.                                                                   |
| **Premium pricing**            | Leads to higher profit margins and improved reputation.  | Can only be applied to certain target markets. Limits the product's reach.                        |
| **Dynamic pricing**            | High profits and sales as well as high flexibility       | Can lead to customer dissatisfaction and can't be applied to all markets.                         |
| **Competitive pricing**        | Your product aligns with the competition.                | Price may not be low enough to attract customers.                                                 |
| **Contribution pricing**       | Allows flexibility and considers demand.                 | It's difficult to classify certain costs as direct or indirect.                                   |

## Promotion

The publicizing of a product, an organization or an activity in order to increase sales or public awareness. Promotion takes many forms and businesses must ensure they send the right message and that the message reaches the target market.

### Above the line

Above the line promotion is promotion aimed at mass markets or an entire population. This may include:

- TV advertising
- Newspaper advertising
- Magazine advertising
- Radio advertising
- Outdoor advertising

| Method                    | Benefits                                                                               | Limitations                                                   |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TV advertising**        | High visual impact and national global reach.                                          | Expensive and TV adverts reach a smaller group nowadays.      |
| **Newspaper advertising** | Can include lots of information and customers can refer to it later.                   | Weak visual impact and newspapers aren't very read anymore.   |
| **Magazine advertising**  | [[Segmentation\|Psychographic segmentation]] is possible and imagery can be effective. | Static imagery may make visual impact less effective.         |
| **Radio advertising**     | [[Segmentation\|Geographic segmentation]] is possible.                                 | There is no images and purely audio and it is less effective. |
| **Outdoor advertising**   | Well-placed adverts can be seen by thousands of people.                                | Static imagery may make visual impact less effective.         |

### Below the line

Below the line promotion is aimed at specific people and not aimed towards the mass media. This may include:

- **Direct marketing**: Sending messages to a customer's email, phone, etc.
- **Sales promotion**: Includes discounts, offers, free samples, etc.
- **Loyalty cards**: Offers customers rewards for buying repeatedly. 
- **After-sales service**: Keeping in touch with a customer after they purchase through warranties, installations, advice and repairs.
- **Public relations**: Improve the business' reputation by doing good things like supporting charitable causes and maintaining good links with the local community.
- **Merchandising**: Creating merch for people to buy such as shirts, pens, mugs, etc.
- **Exhibitions and trade fairs**: Showing up to an expo can help a business promote their products.

| Method                          | Benefits                                                           | Limitations                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Direct marketing**            | Allows for large and custom information to be sent to a customer.  | Many people think it will be spam and won't read the messages. They may also think it's intrusive. |
| **Sales promotion**             | High impact on sales and on customers as a whole.                  | The message may be lost if the competition is doing the same thing.                                |
| **Loyalty cards**               | Data on a customer's spending habits can be collected.             | The discounts offered in the loyalty card may lower profits in the long run.                       |
| **After-sales service**         | Increases customer satisfaction which can lead to greater loyalty. | Increases costs and may require more staff that needs to be [[Training\|trained]].                 |
| **Public relations**            | Improves the brand's reputation.                                   | Employees also need to be [[Training\|trained]] to keep positive community relationships.          |
| **Merchandising**               | Products gain more awareness and consumers become walking ads.     | It is costly to produce and there is no guarantee that consumers will buy it.                      |
| **Exhibitions and trade fairs** | Attracts new potential customers.                                  | The competitions is right there, too.                                                              |

### Through the line

Through the line promotion involves using a combination of above the line and below the line activities. By using both channels, a company can gain exposure for a brand or product as well as achieving sales and attract customers.

- **Digital marketing**: Due to developments in technology, custom advertisements can be sent to lots of people very quickly.
- **360-degree marketing**: An approach to advertising that involves using all methods described above to reinforce brand image and generate sales.

### Social media

There are many social media platforms, including Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, that are used by people all over the world. Businesses can use these platforms to communicate with potential customers through multiple means.

- **Paid advertisements**: Social media platforms use the data provided by the users of the platform, along with their daily activity, to show personalized advertisements to people.
- **Content generated by users**: Businesses can share content generated by other users of social media. A business can, for example, retweet a comment posted voluntarily by a user.
- **One-to-one messages**: Businesses can use social media to deliver customer service through private messaging. Consumers may appreciate this personalized service.
- **Influencers**: Influencers are famous people who can persuade a lot of people. Followers of influencers trust their judgement and many businesses hire influencers to increase the sales of their products.

Advantages of using social media includes low costs, more knowledge on customers, a better customer service and easy ways to measure a company's reputation. However, a business that executes poor social media marketing can harm the company quickly.

## Place

How products travel from producers to consumers. Some producers may sell directly to consumers. All businesses must consider how their products are distributed because this is a critical part of meeting consumer needs and expectations.

Businesses can sell a product directly to consumers or get an intermediary. Aka, a middle-man, turning direct distribution into indirect distribution. This person or entity can be one of the following:

- **Wholesaler**: They purchase large amounts of a product from a business and then sell it to retailers and sometimes customers.
- **Retailers**: They purchase a product and sell them to consumers. This can include supermarkets, convenience stores, vending machines, etc.
- **Agents**: An agent may be used to aid any of the distribution channels explained above. Agents promote and sell products on behalf of producers.

| Channel                                        | Benefits                                                                                            | Limitations                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Direct distribution                            | Eliminates intermediary expenses and increases contact with the customer, which can be appreciated. | Reduces distribution options and you need to hire more staff, which is expensive.            |
| Producer to retailer to consumer               | Easy distribution of the product and consumers can try the product.                                 | The intermediary keeps some profit and you lose control over the products.                   |
| Producer to wholesaler to retailer to consumer | Reduces advertising costs from the equation and gives possibility of entering new markets.          | Another intermediary keeps more profit and you have practically no control over the product. |
| Agents                                         | Agents have knowledge on the legal aspects of more countries, so you can enter those markets.       | A commission is paid to the agent and producers have no control over the product either.     |

## People

Everyone who works in the service business and has contact with customers. The friendliness, helpfulness and problem-solving abilities of employees are critically important for a business to satisfy its customers.

It's important to recognize that different cultures may have different needs and expectations of people. These may include:

- The importance of small talk.
- The need for special treatment for longer established customers.
- The need for direct communication.
- The importance of hierarchy and respect for positions of authority.
- Punctuality and timeliness.
- The importance of rules and systems.

>[!success]
>A good strategy is to [[Training|train]] staff to deal with all types of customers.

## Process

How a service is delivered to customers including how a customer places and pays for an order, the delivery systems and customer feedback. When these processes work smoothly, customers hardly notice them. When processes go badly, customers may leave dissatisfied, even if other elements of the marketing mix are excellent.

Processes may include:

- **Placing and paying for orders**: Businesses must have secure methods for placing and paying for orders. However, placing orders quickly can also be appreciated and a unique selling point. If it's too cumbersome to order an item, customers may leave the process and leave unsatisfied.
- **Delivery systems**: Businesses should be quick and efficient when delivering the product to its customer. If a florist can deliver bouquets of flowers at short notice, then that florist is more likely to be chosen over its rivals.
- **Customer feedback**: Customer feedback allows businesses to maintain and improve their service levels. Businesses can create processes to gather feedback.
- **Afer-sales service**: This refers to maintenance, help and information a business provides to the customer after they have purchased a product. It's especially important in the service sector.

## Physical evidence

Every tangible aspect of the service, that is, all the physical things a customer sees and experiences. The visual, spatial, auditory and olfactory elements can have an enormous impact on how a customer perceives an experience and their satisfaction levels.

>[!success]
>Positive physical evidence factors can become a Unique Selling Point (USP) if they are executed very well.

>[!example]
>Imagine going to a cinema where the staff look scruffy, the theatre floor is covered with litter and the toilets are not clean. Even though these things have nothing to do with the quality of the film you are watching, they are likely to affect your experience.
