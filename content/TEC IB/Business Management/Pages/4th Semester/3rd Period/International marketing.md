International marketing is the process of marketing goods and services in more than one country. Businesses can enter international markets in different ways with various outcomes in terms of control over marketing activities, risk, and costs.

Opportunities of international marketing include:

- Larger pool of customers and greater sales revenue.
- Lower costs of production and [[Economies of scale|economies of scale]].
- If the company fails abroad, it operates normally in its home country.
- Improved brand reputation.

However, threats may be induced by the [[STEEPLE analysis|STEEPLE]] factors.

# Entry strategies

- **Exporting**: Involves selling products in other countries and sending them there. It can be either direct or indirect.
	- **Direct**: When a business sends their own products overseas through agents, distributors or direct marketing and online sales.
	- **Indirect**: When a business or an exporting agency purchases products from a country with the purpose of trading those products overseas.
- **Franchising**: When a business sells the permission to use the name and business model to someone else who is in another country. There is a high degree of control over the marketing of the company's products.
- **Licensing**: A business sells the license of a business to another person. The other person can then run the business without much oversight.
- **Direct investment**: This is a long-term investment in a foreign country by a [[Multinational companies (MNCs)|multinational company]] and involves setting up factories and distribution facilities in the foreign country.
- **[[Joint venture|Joint ventures]]**: Two companies are joined together to create a new, larger company. International joint ventures provide a route of entry into markets where the government restricts foreign ownership.
- **[[Business merger|Mergers]] and [[Acquisition|acquisitions]]**: Mergers and acquisitions are ways for two companies to extend into international markets by using the companies already existing there.
- **[[Takeover|Takeovers]]**: When a business buys another business' shares and now owns that company. Same thing as mergers and acquisitions, but more forceful.
