The complex conjugates theorem is a rule that states that when getting the roots or solutions of a polynomial of any degree, if all of its coefficients are real, then the [[Complex numbers|complex]] roots of the equation will always come in [[Complex numbers#Not-so basic operations|conjugate]] pairs.

For example, consider the following equation.

$$
x^{2}+1=0
$$

We know from the [[Fundamental theorem of algebra|fundamental theorem of algebra]] that this equation must have two solutions. You can isolate $x$ to get the answers.

$$
x^{2}=-1
$$
$$
x=\sqrt{ -1 }
$$

We know  that the imaginary unit is equal to $\sqrt{ -1 }$. Therefore, this equation has two solutions.

$$
x_{1}=i
$$
$$
x_{2}=-i
$$

As you can see, $x_{1}$ is the conjugate of $x_{2}$. This is true for all equations with complex numbers.
