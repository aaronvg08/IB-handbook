The fundamental theorem of algebra is not how algebra started, but it does say something interesting about polynomials. It states that any polynomial of degree $n$ has $n$ roots, or solutions. So, for instance, consider the following [[Function|functions]].

$$
f(x)=x^{4}+5x+1
$$
$$
g(x)=4x^{3}+x+30
$$
$$
p(x)=x{^2}+5x-5
$$

The fundamental theorem of algebra states that $f(x)$, since it is of degree 4, must have 4 roots or solutions. The same is true for the rest. For $g(x)$, it has 3 roots, and $p(x)$ has 2 roots. 

>[!warning]
>These roots may not always be real. Some roots can be [[Complex numbers|imaginary]]!
