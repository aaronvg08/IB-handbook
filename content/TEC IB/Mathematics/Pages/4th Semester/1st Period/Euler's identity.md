Euler's identity is a rule in [[Complex numbers|complex numbers]] that connects $e$, $i$, and $\pi$ in an elegant and understandable way. It is one of the most famous examples of mathematical beauty, and a celebrated identity in the fandom. It is also connected to [[Euler's formula]]. The identity is usually notated like so.

$$
e^{\pi i}+1=0
$$

Which therefore states the following.

$$
e^{\pi i}=-1
$$

This is the main argument of the identity: that $e^{\pi i}=-1$.

>[!note]
>A consequence of this identity is that $\ln(-1)=\pi i$, as seen in [[Logarithm|logarithms]].
