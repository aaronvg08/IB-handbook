Complex numbers are an expansion of numbers that define negative square roots. Complex numbers all stem from the imaginary unit, which is the following.

$$
i=\sqrt{ -1 }
$$

We don't know what $i$ is. After all, real numbers cannot explain it. But imaginary numbers can, which is what we call numbers that have some operation done on them by $i$.

# Operations

## Basic operations

Basic operations, such as addition, subtraction, multiplication and division can be done to complex numbers just like you would to a real number. 

- **Addition & subtraction**: Do in [[Cartesian form|cartesian form ]]and then later convert if you need it. You would add as if $i$ is a variable. Add real with real, and imaginary with imaginary.
	- $(a+bi)\pm(c+di)=(a\pm c)+(b\pm d)i$
- **Multiplication**: Can be done in [[Polar form (math)|polar]] and [[Euler's form]], then convert if necessary. You usually multiply the magnitudes and add the angles.
	- **Polar form**: $[M_{1}(\cos \theta_{1}+i\sin \theta_{1})][M_{2}(\cos \theta_{2}+i\sin \theta_{2})]=M_{1} M_{2}(\cos(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})+i\sin(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}))$
	- **Euler's form**: $(M_{1}\cdot e^{\theta_{1}i})(M_{2}\cdot e^{\theta_{2}i})=M_{1}M_{2}\cdot e^{(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})i}$
- **Division**: Can be done in [[Polar form (math)|polar]] and [[Euler's form]], then convert if necessary. You usually divide the magnitudes and subtract the angles.
	- **Polar form**: $M_{1}(\cos \theta_{1}+i\sin \theta_{2}) \div M_{2}(\cos \theta_{1}+i\sin \theta_{2})=\frac{M_{1}}{M_{2}}(\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+i\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))$
	- **Euler's form**: $M_{1}\cdot e^{\theta_{1}i}\div M_{2}\cdot e^{\theta_{2}i}=\frac{M_{1}}{M_{2}}\cdot e^{(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})i}$

## Not-so basic operations

Other operations are also possible when working with complex numbers that you usually cannot apply to norma numbers, or they work differently with complex ones.

- **Real part**: Get the real part of the complex number.
	- $\mathrm{Re}(5+3i)=5$
- **Imaginary part**: Get the imaginary part of the complex number.
	- $\mathrm{Im}(5+3i)=3$
- **Absolute value**: Get the magnitude of the complex number.
	- $|3-4i|=\sqrt{ 3^{2}+(-4i)^{2} }\to|5+3i|=\sqrt{ 25 }\to|5+3i|=5$
- **Conjugate**: See [[Conjugate|conjugate]] for more info.
	- $\overline{3-4i}=3+4i$
- **Powers**: Use [[De Moivre's theorem]].
