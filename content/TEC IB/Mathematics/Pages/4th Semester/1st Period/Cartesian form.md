The cartesian form is a way to represent [[Complex numbers|complex numbers]] by separating the real component from the imaginary component. The cartesian form has the following format.

$$
z=a+bi
$$

Where $z$ is a complex number. An example of a complex number in cartesian form would look something like this.

$$
5+3i
$$

In the [[Argand plane|argand plane]], the cartesian form represents how many steps you go to left or right in the $x$ axis, and how many steps you take up and down in the $y$ axis.

# Converting

Converting from the cartesian form into [[Euler's form]] is very easy. All we have to do is calculate the magnitude ($M$) and the angle ($\theta$) components.

You can get either with the following formulas.

$$
M=\sqrt{ a^{2} +b^{2}}
$$
$$
\theta=\arctan\left( \frac{b}{a} \right)
$$

## Example

Consider the following number in cartesian form.

$$
4+2i
$$

We get the magnitude of this number.

$$
M=\sqrt{ 4^{2}+2^{2} }
$$
$$
M=2\sqrt{ 5 }
$$
$$
M\approx4.47
$$

Then, the angle (in [[Radians|radians]])!

$$
\theta=\arctan\left( \frac{2}{4} \right)
$$
$$
\theta \approx 0.46
$$

And now we just assemble the final result.

$$
4+2i=4.47\cdot e^{0.46i}
$$

Now, since we have the magnitude and the angle, we can also represent this number in [[Polar form (math)|polar form]].

$$
4.47(\cos 0.46+i\sin 0.46)
$$
$$
4.47\;\text{cis}\;0.46
$$
