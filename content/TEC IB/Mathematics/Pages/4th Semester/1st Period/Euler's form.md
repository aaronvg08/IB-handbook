Euler's form is a way to represent [[Complex numbers|complex numbers]] by indicating the magnitude of the number, and the degrees for which it is rotated. It is highly related to [[Polar form (math)|polar form]], as well as [[Euler's formula]], and has the following format.

$$
z=M\cdot e^{\theta i}
$$

Where $M$ is the magnitude and $\theta$ is the angle in [[Radians|radians]]. An example of a complex number in Euler's form would look something like this.

$$
2e^{\frac{\pi}{3}i}
$$

In the [[Argand plane|argand plane]], Euler's form represents how big the line would be to draw from the point $(0,0)$ to the line, and what angle you would need to aim at in order to hit that point.

# Converting

Converting from Euler's form into [[Cartesian form|cartesian form]] is very easy. All we have to do is calculate the $a$ and $b$ components.

You can get either with the following formulas.

$$
a=M\cdot \cos (\theta)
$$
$$
b=M\cdot \sin (\theta)
$$

## Example

Consider the following number in Euler's form.

$$
3e^{-2i}
$$

You get the $a$ component.

$$
a=3\cos(-2)
$$
$$
a\approx-1.24
$$

Then, you get the $b$ component.

$$
b=3\sin(-2)
$$
$$
b=-2.72
$$

And now we just assemble the final result.

$$
3e^{-2i}=-1.24-2.72i
$$
