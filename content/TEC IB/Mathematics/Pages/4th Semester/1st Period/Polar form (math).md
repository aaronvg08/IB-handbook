The polar form is a way to represent [[Complex numbers|complex numbers]] by indicating the magnitude of the number, and the degrees for which it is rotated. It is highly related to [[Euler's form]] and has the following format.

$$
z=M(\cos \theta+i\sin \theta)
$$

Where $M$ is the magnitude and $\theta$ is the angle in [[Radians|radians]]. An example of a complex number in polar form would look something like this.

$$
2\left( \cos \frac{\pi}{3}+i\sin \frac{\pi}{3} \right)
$$

There is also an additional notation for indicating the $\cos$ and $\sin$ inside the parenthesis, called the $\text{cis}$.

$$
2\left( \cos \frac{\pi}{3}+i\sin \frac{\pi}{3} \right)=2\;\text{cis}\;\frac{\pi}{3}
$$

In the [[Argand plane|argand plane]], the polar form represents how big the line would be to draw from the point $(0,0)$ to the line, and what angle you would need to aim at in order to hit that point.

# Converting

Converting from polar form into [[Cartesian form|cartesian]] form is very easy. All we have to do is calculate the $a$ and $b$ components.

You can get either with the following formulas.

$$
a=M\cdot \cos (\theta)
$$
$$
b=M\cdot \sin (\theta)
$$

## Example

Consider the following number in polar form.

$$
2\left( \cos \frac{\pi}{4}+i\sin \frac{\pi}{4} \right)=2\;\text{cis}\; \frac{\pi}{4}
$$

You get the $a$ component.

$$
a=2\cos(\frac{\pi}{4})
$$
$$
a=\sqrt{ 2 }
$$

Then, you get the $b$ component.

$$
b=2\sin\left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right)
$$
$$
b=\sqrt{ 2 }
$$

And now we just assemble the final result.

$$
2\left( \cos \frac{\pi}{4}+i\sin \frac{\pi}{4} \right)=\sqrt{ 2 }+\sqrt{ 2 }i
$$
