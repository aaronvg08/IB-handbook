The standard deviation is the king of the dispersion measures. It is the representation of how far away the data is from the [[Arithmetic mean|mean]]. A high standard deviation means that the data is very far, while a low one means that it's close together.

The standard deviation is calculated by taking the [[Root mean square (RMS)|root mean square]] of all the [[Deviation|deviations]] in a dataset. The following formula is the formula for the population.

$$
\sigma=\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x-\mu)^{2} }
$$

And the following formula is for a sample.

$$
\sigma=\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x-\mu)^{2} }
$$

Where $n$ is the number of values, $x$ is a value in the set and $\mu$ is the [[Arithmetic mean|average]] of the dataset.

For grouped data, you need to get the [[Variance|variance]] first, and then do it to the square root to get the standard deviation, as seen in the formula below.

$$
\sigma=\sqrt{ \sigma^{2} }
$$
