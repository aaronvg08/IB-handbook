The unit circle is a very important [[Circle|circle]] in [[Trigonometry|trigonometry]] since it tells you something very important when it comes to [[Trigonometric ratios|trigonometric ratios]].

It states that, if you were to draw a circle on a cartesian plane with a radius of 1, then any point on that circle represents the values of $(\cos x,\sin x)$.

![[unit circle.png|center|400]]

For instance, this would mean that $\cos \pi=-1$ and $\sin \pi=0$ in [[Radians|radians]], and that $\cos 180°=-1$ and $\sin 180°=0$ in [[Degrees|degrees]].
