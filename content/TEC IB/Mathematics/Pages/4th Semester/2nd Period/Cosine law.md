The cosine law is a rule in [[Trigonometry|trigonometry]] that is used to obtain the angles and sides of a triangle. Unlike when using [[Trigonometric ratios|trigonometric ratios]], it doesn't have to be a right triangle for it to work. The law is as follows:

$$
A^{2}=B^{2}+C^{2}-2BC\cdot \cos(\alpha)
$$
$$
B^{2}=A^{2}+C^{2}-2AC\cdot \cos(\beta)
$$
$$
C^{2}=A^{2}+B^{2}-2AB\cdot \cos(\gamma)
$$

It basically states that a side's length is given by the sum of the squares of the other two sides, minus the product of both sides times two and times the cosine of the angle.

>[!tip]
>You should use cosine law when one of the following applies.
>- You have two sides and the angle between them.
>- You have all three sides.
