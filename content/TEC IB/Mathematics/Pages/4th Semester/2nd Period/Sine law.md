The sine law is a rule in [[Trigonometry|trigonometry]] that is used to obtain the angles and sides of a triangle. Unlike when using [[Trigonometric ratios|trigonometric ratios]], it doesn't have to be a right triangle for it to work. The law is as follows:

$$
\frac{A}{\sin \alpha}=\frac{B}{\sin \beta}=\frac{C}{\sin \gamma}
$$

Where $A, B,C$ are sides of a triangle, and $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ are all the angles opposite to those sides.

>[!tip]
>You should use sine law when one of the following applies.
>- You have two angles and one side.
>- You have two sides and the angle opposite one of them

# Ambiguous case

The ambiguous case for the sine law is a situation where the sine law can yield two different results for two different [[Triangle|triangles]]. It usually happens when the calculation involves two sides and one opposite angle. In order to get the second result from ambiguous case, we subtract 180 from the original angle

$$
\angle A=180-\theta
$$

Where $\angle A$ is the new angle and $\theta$ is the old angle.

>[!note]
>If the angle that you get from sine law doesn't fit the diagram or situation (is more than 90° or such), you should probably check the ambiguous case.
