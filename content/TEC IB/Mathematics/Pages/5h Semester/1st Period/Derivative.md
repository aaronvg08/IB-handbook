The derivative in [[Calculus|calculus]] is a way to describe how the [[Slope|slope]] of a [[Function|function]] changes over time. It does so by creating a different function based on the previous one that gives you the slope of the [[Tangent (calculus)|tangent]] at a specific point. There are two distinct notations for the derivative, as seen below.

$$
f(x)=x{^2}+3x-5
$$
$$
f'(x)=2x+3
$$
$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=2x+3
$$

>[!Question] Differentiate
>To differentiate means to get the derivative. To get the gradient of a function at a point also involves getting the derivative.

# Rules

To get the derivative of a function, there are many rules you can follow both to make your life easier with complex functions or to quickly get the derivative. Here are the rules you need:

- [[Power rule]]
- [[Constant rule]]
- [[Chain rule]]
- [[Product rule]]
- [[Quotient rule]]
- [[Euler's exponent rule]]

>[!note]
>There are thousands of rules out there! These are only the ones that the IB recognizes.

# Increasing & decreasing

When the derivative, and therefore, the [[Tangent (calculus)|tangent line]]'s slope is positive in a certain place, it means that the function is increasing at that point. Therefore, when it is negative, it is decreasing at a point. This can be seen below.
