The reciprocal rule in [[Calculus|calculus]] is a rule for [[Integral|integrating]] functions where the exponent of $x$ is -1. Normally if you use [[Power rule|power rule]], you would get a division by zero. However, the reciprocal rule saves you.

$$
\int \frac{1}{x} \, dx=\ln|x|+C 
$$

If you have a $ax+b$ form, use the following instead.

$$
\int \frac{1}{ax+b} \, dx=\frac{1}{a}\ln|ax+b|+C 
$$

>[!note]
>Remember that $\frac{1}{x}=x^{-1}$.
