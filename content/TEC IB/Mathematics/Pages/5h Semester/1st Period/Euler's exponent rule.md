Euler's exponent rule in [[Calculus|calculus]] is one of the most interesting rules in the world of math. It states that the [[Derivative|derivative]] of a [[Function|function]] in the form of exponential with Euler's number is itself, which also means that the [[Integral|integral]] of such a function is also itself.

$$
\frac{d}{dx}e^{ x }=e^{ x }
$$
$$
\int e^{ x } \, dx=e^{ x } +C
$$

For integration, if you have a $ax+b$ format, use this one instead.

$$
\int e^{(ax+b)} \, dx=\frac{1}{a}e^{(ax+b)}+C 
$$

>[!note]
>This is the only function who is its own derivative!
