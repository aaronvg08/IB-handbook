The power rule in [[Calculus|calculus]] is the most fundamental rule to [[Derivative|differentiate]] and [[[Integral|integrate]] a [[Function|function]]. You use it for almost everything, and it is the ABC of calculus. 

# Derivative

The rule states that to differentiate anything with an $x$, you multiply the exponent by the coefficient and lower the exponent by 1.

$$
f(x)=x{^n}
$$
$$
f'(x)=nx^{n-1}
$$

# Integral

The rule states that to integrate anything with an $x$, you divide the whole thing by the exponent plus one.

$$
\int x^{n} \, dx=\frac{x^{(n+1)}}{n+1} +C
$$

If you have a form of $ax+b$, use the following instead.

$$
\int (ax+b)^{n} \, dx=\frac{1}{a} \frac{x^{(n+1)}}{n+1} +C
$$

>[!warning]
>This works as long as $n \neq -1$. If $n=-1$, use the [[Reciprocal rule|reciprocal rule]].
