The sigma notation is a mathematical way to represent a sum of similar numbers using the Greek letter sigma ( $\Sigma$ ). It can also be used to represent sequences. A sum represented through sigma notation might look something like this:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k $$

> [!info]
> We use the capital letter sigma, $\Sigma$, for this. The lower case sigma looks like this: $\sigma$.

# Elements

A sum with sigma notation is very easy to understand. It basically represents a sum of elements that have something in common.

![[sigma notation.png|center|600]]

- **Index**: The variable that represents the start of the progression.
- **Upper bound**: The last number the progression reaches before stopping
- **Lower bound**: The value where i starts off from.
- **Variable expression**: Everything we do to the value before summing it.

# Developing sums

## Example 1

So, for example, take this:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{5} (k+2) $$

This sum is able to be developed like so.

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{5} (k+2) = (1+2) + (2+2) + (3+2) + (4+2) + (5+2)
$$
$$= 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 $$

$$ = 25 $$

## Example 2

$$
\sum_{k=2}^{5}{\frac{1}{2{^k}}}=\frac{1}{2{^2}}+\frac{1}{2{^3}}+\frac{1}{2{^4}}+\frac{1}{2{^5}}
$$
$$
=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{16}+\frac{1}{32}
$$
$$
=\frac{15}{32}
$$
