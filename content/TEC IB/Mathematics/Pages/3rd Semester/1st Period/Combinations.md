Variations are a type of [[Combinations and permutations|combinatoric]]. They are a way to count the possible arrangements in a set where repetition isn't allowed and order doesn't matter.

$$
aCb=\binom{a}{b}=\frac{a!}{b!(a-b)!}
$$
- $a$: Number of elements.
- $b$: Number of subsets.
- $aCb$: Combinations of $a$ elements in $b$ subsets.

>[!tip]
>The second term, $\binom{a}{b}$, is written with binomial coefficient notation. It can be inserted like that onto your calculator for quick calculations.

# With repetition

This is a special flavor of combinations where repetition is allowed, yet order still doesn't matter. The formula is below.

$$
aCRb=\binom{n+b-1}{b}=\frac{(n+b-1)!}{b!(n-1)!}
$$
- $a$: Number of elements.
- $b$: Number of subsets.
- $aCRb$: Combinations with repetition of $a$ elements in $b$ subsets.
