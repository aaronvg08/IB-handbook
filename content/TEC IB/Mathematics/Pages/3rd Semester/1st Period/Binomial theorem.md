The binomial theorem is an equation that can be used to [[Binomial expansion|expand binomials]] really easily. The binomial theorem is defined as such:

$$
(a+b){^n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r}a^{n-r}b{^r}
$$
Where $\binom{n}{r}$ basically means:

$$
\binom{n}{r}=\frac{n!}{(n-r)!r!}
$$

> [!tip] 
> You can usually plug $\binom{n}{r}$ into your calculator to avoid doing all that. Remember that!
# Example

For a binomial like the one below,

$$
(a+b){^2}
$$

We expand like such:

$$
(a+b){^2}=\binom{2}{0}a{^{2-0}}b{^0}+\binom{2}{1}a{^{2-1}}b{^1}+\binom{2}{2}a{^{2-2}}b{^2}
$$
$$
(a+b){^2}=\frac{2!}{(2-0)!0!}a{^{2-0}}b{^0}+\frac{2!}{(2-1)!1!}a{^{2-1}}b{^1}+\frac{2!}{(2-2)!2!}a{^{2-2}}b{^2}
$$
$$
(a+b){^2}=a{^2}+2ab+b{^2}
$$

> [!note]
> We just proved the $(a+b){^2}=a{^2}+2ab+2b{^2}$ rule!
