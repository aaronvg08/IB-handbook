A quadratic function is a type of [[Function|function]] that, as its name indicates, comes from a quadratic equation. Therefore, they look something like this:

$$
f(x)=ax{^2}+bx+c
$$
# Graph

The graph of a quadratic function is called a parabola. Consider the following function.

$$
f(x)=x{^2}-4
$$

This function, when graphed, looks something like this:

![[quadratic function.png|center|450]]

The function, because quadratic equations have two solutions, splits into two and becomes a parabola. Because $a>0$, the parabola goes upwards. Otherwise, it would open downwards.

The axis of symmetry is the middle point where the function "splits". To get the axis of symmetry, you can use the following formula.

$$
x=-\frac{b}{2a}
$$
The axis of symmetry is, therefore, the x-coordinate of where the vertex is located. The vertex is the blue point in the diagram, and the point where the function starts branching off.

Finally, the roots are the two places where the parabola collides with the x-axis. These roots represent the solution to the quadratic equation. However, not all quadratic equations have two solutions, or any solutions at all. For this, the discriminant exists. You can get the discriminant from the following formula.

$$
\Delta=b{^2}-4ac
$$

Then, you can interpret the result to find the number of roots:

- $\Delta>0$: Two distinct real roots.
- $\Delta=0$: Only one real root.
- $\Delta>0$: No real roots (only complex ones).

# Domain & range

- The [[Domain|domain]] for a quadratic function is always $(-\infty,\infty)$, because all inputs are possible.
- The [[Range (functions)|range]] for a linear function depends on the parabola and its direction. If the parabola opens upwards, the range is from the vertex to $\infty$. However, if it opens downwards, the range is from $-\infty$ to the vertex.
