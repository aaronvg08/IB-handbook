The slope, also called the gradient, is the rate of change for a [[Linear function|linear function]] or a line. As its name indicates, it can also be used to understand how the graph of a function behaves.

The slope of a function is given with the following formula.

$$
m=\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}
$$

Where $(x_{1},y_{1})$ and $(x_{2}, y_{2})$ are two points that the function touches.

Some important things to consider with slopes are the following:

- The slope of a vertical line is undefined.
- The slope of a horizontal line (such as a [[Constant function|constant function]]) is always 0.
- The product of the slope of two linear functions which are perpendicular to each other should equal $-1$.
- If two functions are parallel to each other, they have the same slope.
