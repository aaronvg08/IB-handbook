A linear inequality refers to an inequality that represents a relationship between linear equations. So, there is no square, and there are no fractions with variables. A linear inequality may look something like this.

$$
2x+5\geq 5
$$
# Solving

Solving a linear inequality is the same as solving a linear equation. You simply have to pass all terms to the other side to reveal the value of $x$. For instance, take the following example.

$$
5x-2<3
$$

You find $x$ like so.

$$
5x<3+2
$$
$$
5x<5
$$
$$
x< \frac{5}{5}
$$
$$
x<1
$$

And there we have it, $x$ is less than 1. In inequality notation, $x<1$. In [[Interval notation|interval notation]], we can say that $x \in(-\infty, 1)$. In the number line, the area that belongs to $x$ looks like this:
