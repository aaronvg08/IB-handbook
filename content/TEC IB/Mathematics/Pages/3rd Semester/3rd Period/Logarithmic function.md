A logarithmic function is a [[Function|function]] that is the opposite of an [[Exponential function|exponential function]]. As its name implies, it has a [[Logarithm|logarithm]] in it. A logarithmic function may look something like this:

$$
f(x)=\log (2x-5)
$$

# Graph

The graph of a logarithmic function, since we know is the opposite of an exponential function, is the same except reflected along the $y=x$ line.

Consider the following function.

$$
f(x)=\log(x)
$$

This function's graph may look something like this.

![[log function.png|center|450]]


As you can see, it is down-sloping with bigger $x$ values and has an asymptote at $x=0$, since $\log(0)=\text{undefined}$.

# Domain & range

- The [[Domain|domain]] of a logarithmic function is always $\text{Inner function} > 0$.
- The [[Range (functions)|range]] of a logarithmic function can only be found with its graph or inverse.
