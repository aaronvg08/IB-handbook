The logarithm is an operation that is the opposite of an exponent. In essence, it means to what number you must raise a value to get another number. The very definition of a logarithm can be seen below.

$$
a^{c}=b\to \log_{a}(b)=c
$$

In the logarithm format, $a$ represents the base. Aka, the number we are raising, and $b$ represents the argument. Aka, the result of the exponentiation.

>[!note] Base
>The base of a logarithm, when not specified, is 10. However, do not forget there is also the natural logarithm.

# Properties

To solve an exponential equation, which is when $x$ is an exponent, we can use the logarithm and its various properties. These properties are seen below.

- [[Power as coefficient]].
- [[Log addition]].
- [[Log subtraction]].
- [[Base change]].

Some other properties that you should know are the following:

- $\log_{a}(1)=0$ for any base $a>0,a \neq 1$
- $\log_{a}(a)=1$ for any base $a>0,a \neq 1$
- $A^{\log_{A}(N)}=N$ for $N>0$ and $A>0, A \neq 1$

# Natural logarithm

The natural logarithm is simply a logarithm with a base of $e$. Its notation is different, and is usually written as seen below.

$$
\ln(10)
$$

>[!important]
>A rule of natural logarithms is that $\ln(-1)=\pi i$ due to [[Euler's identity]].
