Proof by contradiction is a type of [[Proof|proof]] that assumes the opposite of what the proof states, and shows that this assumption leads to a logical contradiction. Therefore, the original statement must be true.

# Example

Let's prove that $\sqrt{3 }$ is irrational. 

First, we assume that $\sqrt{ 3 }$ is rational, which means that it can be represented as a fraction such as $\frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers with no common factors.

If this is true, then,

$$
\frac{a}{b}=\sqrt{ 3 }
$$

We can square both sides.

$$
\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}=3
$$
$$
a^{2}=3b^{2}
$$

This means that $a^{2}$ is divisible by 3, since the result is being multiplied by 3. This also implies that $a$ is divisible by 3. 

Let $a=3k$ for some integer $k$. We can substitute it in the equation.

$$
(3k)^{2}=3b^{2}
$$

We can divide both sides by 3.

$$
3k^{2}=b^{2}
$$

Now this means that $b^{2}$ is divisible by 3, which implies that $b$ is also divisible by 3. But this contradicts our initial assumption that $a$ and $b$ have no common factors. If both are divisible by 3, then the common factor is 3.

Therefore, our initial assumption must be false, and $\sqrt{ 3 }$ must be irrational.
