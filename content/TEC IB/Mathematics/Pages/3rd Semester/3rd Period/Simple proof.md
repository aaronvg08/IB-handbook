Simple proof is the only [[Proof|proof]] math AA SL sees. It is limited to only proving that one side of an equation is equal to the other side. You are given the result of an equation, and an incomplete equation on the other side. You prove that the left side is equal to the right side. 

Simple proofs are all solved in the same way, but they can be classified in:

- Numerical proof
- Algebraic proof

>[!example]
>To prove that $\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{m^{2}+m}=\frac{1}{m}$ for all $m \neq 0,-1$:
>1. Start with the left-hand side: $\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{m^{2}+m}$.
>2. Find a common denominator: $\frac{m^{2}+m}{(m+1)(m^{2}+m)}+\frac{m+1}{(m+1)(m^{2}+m)}$.
>3. Add the fractions: $\frac{m^{2}+2m+1}{(m+1)(m^{2}+m)}$.
>4. Factor the numerator: $\frac{(m+1)^{2}}{(m+1)(m^{2}+m)}$.
>5. Cancel $(m+1)$ from numerator and denominator: $\frac{m+1}{(m^{2}+m)}$.
>6. Factor out $m$ from the denominator: $\frac{m+1}{m(m+1)}$.
>7. Cancel $(m+1)$ from the numerator and denominator: $\frac{1}{m}$.
>
>Thus, we have shown that $\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{m^{2}+m}=\frac{1}{m}$ for all $m \neq 0,-1$.
