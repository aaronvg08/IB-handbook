- **Why do you think the Aunts are allowed to read and write?**

Because knowledge is power, and if the Aunts are already under duress to train the Handmaids and keep the knowledge away from them, then they can keep it more easily. They also need knowledge to manipulate the Handmaids into becoming true believers.

- **What do you think of the reactions at the Centre to learning that Moira had escaped?**

She became a local legend in the Red Center, and a symbol of hope and revelry to the other Handmaids. The fact that an escape was viable was kept in the Handmaid’s head at all times.

- **Had it occurred to you before Cora’s conversation with the handmaids that the Marthas depended on the Handmaids as well as the Commanders? What do you think this reveals about the Handmaid’s standing in the house? Has it changed your mind about it?**

The fact that the Marthas depend on the Handmaid’s more than I thought did change my mind about my perception of them. I believe that, despite their circumstances and stance in the house, they do hold power over the house members, considering that they all depend on her.

- **Why do you think the handmaids are not allowed to have conversations or friendships with their Commander?**

Because the Handmaids are supposed to be devices, or as Offred says: “Two legged wombs”. They are not supposed to have communication or friendships to maintain control over them, and to not allow a bond between them, because that might lead to conspiracy or rebellion.

- **Do you think Scrabble would be more fun if it were illegal, or less fun?**

Offred mentions that Scrabble used to be a very uninteresting game to her, and that it was only played by children or teenagers. However, now that she has been deprived of literacy and entertainment, it’s very fun to her. Therefore, I believe that because Scrabble is illegal, playing it became more fun.

- **Do you think magazines are trivial now? What would change if they were banned?**

Offred briefly mentions in a flashback that magazines used to be boring to her, mentioning that they used to be things to read when nothing else was available. However, as Gilead took place, we can see that her interests on magazines change now that it’s illegal to own them.
