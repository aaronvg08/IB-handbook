# Power/control

Throughout The Handmaid’s tale, there is a constant theme of power and control, and what role it plays in people’s lives, specially the Handmaids. Aunt Lydia is a prime example of how women are control to become Handmaids or to not rebel in general. The way she uses language to make it more appealing to follow the rules instead of asking questions and rebelling can be seen in the following quote: “Knowledge is temptation. What you don’t know won’t tempt you.” Offred constantly battles with power as well, acknowledging how little she has and trying to keep as most as she can.

# Resistance/rebellion

Moira is one of the main symbols of resistance and rebellion that can be observed in the novel. She is used at the Red Center as a symbol of hope and defiance to the Handmaids, and used to show escaping is a possibility. Furthermore, the illicit meetings with The Commander act as a symbol of rebellion, and so does the games of Scrabble they play, since it promotes literacy. Offred herself battles with the feelings of rebellion and resistance against Aunt Lydia’s teachings, which constantly plague her mind.

# Religion/theocracy

Gilead’s environment has multiple references to theocracy and the use of religion. In Gilead, Offred lets the reader know that other religions aren’t allowed, and Jewish people are asked to convert or to leave Gilead. The novel also discusses prayer, both as a tool to control and maintain hope. The Red Center uses prayer as a way to manipulate people, having the future Handmaids recite them multiple times a day, before and after most things. However, later on, Offred uses prayer to ‘speak’ to god as a way to cope with her worries and loneliness.
