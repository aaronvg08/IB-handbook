# Text types

- Advertisement
- Appeal
- Blog
- Brochure/leaflet
- Cartoon
- Diagram
- Electronic text (social media posts)

# Questions to ask

- What is purpose and context of the text? What subject matter and the viewpoint of the text?
- What are the intentions of the authorial choices?
- What are visual features and how do they work with the text?
- Register (formality), style, tone.
- Analyse: technical and formal features.
- Evaluate: authorial intention and effect on the audience.

# Features

- **Context of reception**: The situation or circumstances in which a text is received or interpreted with significant factors.
- **Context of production**
- **Style**
- **Register:** a variety of language determined
- **Tone**: The attitude of a writer towards a subject or audience. It’s conveyed through word choice, connotation, figurative language, punctuation, sentence structure, pronouns, naming, etc.
    - Ironic, Personal, Impersonal, Serious, Sarcastic, Technical, Critical, etc.

# Charity appeal

- Purpose: to recruit you for good cause or to donate money or time.

## Key features

- **Persuasive** - make reader take action and raise awareness of social issue. Look for persuasive rhetorical features.
- **Pathos** - appealing to emotions such as anger, pity, sympathy or guilt; wanting the reader to response.
- **Hard-hitting** - impacting visuals sometimes with shock-value.
- **Credibility** - need to be trustworthy. Look for information that suggests that your donation will make a difference.
- **Metonymy** - introducing a single individual who represents all those that your donation will help.
- **Direct address** - addressing the reader as ‘you’ to create a string connection, or making visual eye contact.

# Infographics

Visual texts designed to present complex information and data.

## Characteristics

- **Audience** - designed to reach a wide audience; watch for technical language that will indicate a narrower audience.
- **Simplification** - simplifying complex knowledge. Look for summaries, bullet points, images with captions, etc.
- **Illustrations** - symbolize ideas in the text.
- **Structure** - visual narratives, how the sequence is displayed.

# Formula for paragraphs

Point, evidence, analysis, connection to the question, evaluation.

# Do’s and don’ts:

- **Never** say attention-grabbing.
- **Never** say the text ‘talks about x’.
