- **Comment on the images on page 51. What is Marji’s response to what she hears?**

She was shocked, and were are her parents. It’s not something a child should hear or learn, and she’s somewhat aware of this, but throughout the book she constantly asks questions even when others don’t want to tell her the answer.

- **How does the relationships between Marji and Laly appear to change after they both learn the truth?**

They clearly don’t like each other, but Marji’s perception of Laly changes. Marji’s father isn’t a hero, or at least she doesn’t think he’s one, but Laly’s father is. Laly’s father was sent to prison, tortured, and murdered. Marji thinks this makes her superior in a way.

- **According to Marji, what makes a hero?**

Going to prison, being tortured… just suffering in general. The worst someone’s life is the ‘better’ for her.

- **How has war corrupted the innocence of children like Marji?**

It changes her perception in her every-day life. From the games she plays to the books she reads, she starts to accept the horrible reality around her as something normal. Even when people aren’t directly talking to her and explaining her things, she still picks up small clues from conversations and learns; for the better or otherwise.

- **What ethical dilemma does Marji face?**

Marji’s mother tells her to forgive people, but everything around her contradicts this idea. That torturers should be massacred. She doesn’t understand what justice is anymore.
