# Segment 1

- First, it’s worth noting the context of the situation they were talking about. Even when people realized that the man hadn’t died a hero, but of cancer, they didn’t care and continued to demonstrate.
    - In desperate times it’s difficult to maintain hope, which is exactly what they wanted. They were desperate for a martyr, for a symbol of hope.

![[TEC IB/English A/Images/image.png|image.png]]

- Subsequently, there’s the scene at hand. Marji’s family is laughing at the situation, acknowledging the ridiculous nature of it.
    - Humor can be used to cope with hard situations, which is the sentiment displayed on this panel.
- Marji doesn’t understand. Both from the dialogue and her expression. She’s confused as to how such obscure themes can lead to humor.
    - The theme of innocence is once again shown here.
- Afterward, she laughs to try to fit in, yet her family isn’t laughing anymore, hinting at her killing the mood.
    - Despite her parents understanding the context of the situation and why it’s humorous in nature, they know that Marji doesn’t understand, which takes away the humor.
- Finally, Marji is seen reading a book on her bed. The book is called “The Reasons for The Revolution”.
    - From the beginning of the novel up to now, Marji has always tried to understand her surroundings. It wasn’t just because her parents bought her books to “enlighten” her; she showed a genuine desire to understand the situation because she cared, and because she wanted to fit in.

  

# Segment 2

![[TEC IB/English A/Images/image 1.png|image 1.png]]

- The use of white and black backgrounds is interesting in these panels. The first panel is completely white while the others are black, hinting at the first to be inside a building, and the ones below outside.
    - Perhaps the author intended for the reader to understand that while the Shah picks a prime minister, people are rioting right outside his door.

- In the first panel, the Shah goes through multiple prime ministers. However, he doesn’t seem to be paying attention to his surroundings, and he rejects each one for a superficial reason.
    - Despite the Shah promising that he’ll attempt to bring democracy into the country, this panel lets the reader know that he wasn’t genuinely trying or taking the situation seriously.
- The following panels depict the riots currently happening in the country, in the form of the destruction of effigies and statues.
    - The last panel is especially powerful; it depicts the Shah’s effigy engulfed in flames. His expression of happiness is emphasized by the imagery of flames, which is a very powerful way of conveying how the people think of the Shah, how the Shah is supposed to be thought of, and perhaps how the Shah thinks of himself too.
