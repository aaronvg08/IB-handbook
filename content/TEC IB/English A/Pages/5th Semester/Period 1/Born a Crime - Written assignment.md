# Prompt

**Impact and Message:** Analyze the broader impact and message of "Born a Crime" on readers. Consider how Noah's use of humor and personal anecdotes contributes to his overall message. How does the book challenge preconceived notions about race and identity? What lessons can contemporary readers learn from his experiences?
# Essay

Born a Crime is a memoir written by Trevor Noah that discusses his development and life as a mixed person in South Africa. He uses anecdotes and humor to bring attention to mature themes such as systemic racism, domestic abuse, and identity while keeping the tone friendly, which allows him to explain those subjects in as straightforward and engaging way to the reader.

One of the best examples of how Noah makes light of a serious situation without undermining the seriousness of the topic is in one of the first anecdotes in the book. Noah describes how when he was nine years old, in order to save him from malicious people, his mother had to throw him out of a moving car. He ends the anecdote by mentioning something he said: "Look, Mom. I know you love Jesus, but maybe next week you could ask him to meet us at our house. Because this really wasn’t a fun night." This inherently humorous way of ending the anecdote not only adds a lighter tone to the rather serious accident, but also emphasizes the innocence of a young child who doesn't fully understand the circumstances of his situation. 

Furthermore, Noah uses this type of storytelling to challenge notions regarding race and identity. In one of the chapters, Noah explains how he had a friend named Hitler who he constantly invited to dance in his DJ business. He elaborates on this by explicitly mentioning people's unawareness of the historical connotation of the name, as shown in the following quote: "We’d have the whole crowd in a frenzy, a thousand people in the street chanting along with their hands in the air. 'Go Hit-ler! Go Hit-ler! Go Hit-ler! Go Hit-ler!'" By doing this, Noah challenges the idea or assumption that everyone in the world knows about the holocaust and about its implications, when in reality, it might not be as well-taught in other countries such as South Africa, just like how Apartheid might not be very well known in other countries.
