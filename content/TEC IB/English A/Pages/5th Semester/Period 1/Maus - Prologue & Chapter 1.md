- **What is your first impression of Vladek Spiegelman? What do you ponder about his remarks about friends?**

He looks like a really crude person who is affected by the war. I can assume that he said that because he was in the holocaust. He may be distrustful of people now if he considers that you can only know which people are your friends in an extreme situation.

She committed suicide. It's obviously taken a toll on the entire family, specially Vladek, because he re-married but I don't think he likes Mala.

- **On page 12 we see a close-up of Vladek as he pedals his exercise bicycle. What is the meaning of the numbers tattooed on his arms? How does this single image manage to convey information that might occupy paragraphs of text?**

The close-up of his arm is a number tattooed on his arm. This is a number placed on him from the holocaust, since the jewish people were classified in Auschwitz. That panel reflects back on that time and juxtaposes it in the present, with the tattoo representing how there are still both physical and mental remains (or 'scars') of what occurred in the war. It is communicated using only an image instead of text since the book is a graphic novel, which adds complexity to the story in the form of images.
