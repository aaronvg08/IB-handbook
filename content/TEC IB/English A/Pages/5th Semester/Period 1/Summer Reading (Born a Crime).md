# Part I

## Chapter 1 - Run

- I like my plotline like I like my coffee: thrown in my face. And there’s no other way this book could’ve caught my attention other than by telling me about getting thrown out of moving cars.
- I’m glad Trevor enjoyed going to church, and I get what he means. I used to go to church too, and I’d take very long to get back home as well. There were two churches I went to: one that was near my house (for when I got lucky), and the other which was an hour away. Worst case scenario it would take three hours for me to come back home, and that was a drag for me. I cannot imagine spending more than 8 hours going to church and then back. Praise the lord and all that, but that sounds awful.
- His mom is maybe too stubborn for her own good.
- Woah, I’m actually so sorry for Trevor. He was so young, barely a kid, and he was thrown out of a moving bus. And to save his life. Must’ve been a rough childhood.

## Chapter 2 - Born a Crime

- Nevermind, I actually like Trevor’s mom. What bravery it must’ve taken to do all that she did under her circumstances.
- I love when the titles of books mean something or are included in the book. Though, it’s unfortunate he felt like his existence was a crime, and it’s even worse that it was true.
- I find it really extreme that police were sent to people’s houses to spy on them. I guess that goes to show the lengths the government went to try to keep apartheid working.
- I love Trevor’s way of writing. It feels like talking to a friend.

## Chapter 3 - Trevor, Pray

- I feel kind of bad laughing at this book, because it really is hilarious at times, when it talks about how awful it was living where he did.
- Poor Trevor, he just wanted to do his necessities in peace.

## Chapter 4 - Chameleon

- Interesting how racist behaviors, such as treating white people better, is reinforced in black or mixed families. That really must speak of how deep-rooted apartheid was in families. Except for Trevor’s mom. She really thought differently and did a lot of things differently despite her environment and the time.
- Woah, Trevor’s mom knows so many languages. I know it’s mostly out of necessity, but that’s impressive.

## Chapter 5 - The Second Girl

- Trevor’s mom went through a lot. I’m sorry for her. It must’ve been awful being unwanted by your entire family and ending up where she did.
- I agree with Trevor’s mom on how he treated Trevor. I never understood why people speak to children like they’re stupid. They’re not stupid, they’re just new to the world. If you treat them like they’re stupid, then they will be. I’m glad Trevor wasn’t treated like that.
- I’m glad that, despite the fact that they both had a terrible time in Soweto, once they left they actually had fun there.
- What an amazing mom he had.

## Chapter 6 - Loopholes

- He was laughing because the people at his school hit him too soft. That’s hilarious.
- Oh he actually burned a house down. How unlucky for Abel. I know he didn’t mean it but it’s kind of crazy that he didn’t get into that much trouble.
- I’m with Mlungisi. How do you get so far as to burn a house down, even if by accident, and don’t get disowned.

## Chapter 7 - Fufi

- Poor cats. Also that sounds like a terrible sight to come home to.
- I actually prefer cats than dogs. I think it’s good that they don’t require that much attention and are just okay doing things by themselves.
- No way. They didn’t figure Fufi was deaf before she died, they just thought she was stupid. But she doesn’t sound stupid to me. She could do tricks and climb a five feet wall. A stupid dog just sits there staring at nothing until they come back.
- Oh Fufi had a double life. At least it taught him a valuable lesson.

## Chapter 8 - Robert

- His father was never racist either. Trevor really did grow up with the best influences the world could arrange for him.
- “When an African dad buys his kid a present, the last thing he’s going to do is give some fat white man credit for it.” That’s hilarious, but I get it.
- I understand because my dad is like that as well. All I know about him and his family and where and how he grew up I got from tiny little stories and bits of information, like a jigsaw. I still don’t know much about him, but I know he grew up in a place where he was destined to stay there, and despite all the odds, made it out.
- I think it’s unfortunate that Trevor had to eventually stop seeing his father due to circumstances, but at least I’m glad that he found him, and that he cared to do so to begin with.
- Aw, I’m really glad that his father really was involved in his life, even as an spectator and not a direct participant.
