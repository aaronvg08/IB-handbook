# Elements

- **Masthead** - Name of the newspaper and date of publication.
- **Headline** - How the specific article is titled. The headlines can create the tone or the angle of a story with the words.
- **Visuals** - Tabloids have larger photographs to add to the sensationalism while broadsheet newspaper use smaller pictures. How can a photograph have bias?
- **Copy** - Main text, excluding the masthead and the headline.

## Headline features

- **Alliteration** - Catchy.
    - “Hurricanes Leave Hondurans Homeless and Destitute”
- **Word Play / Puns** - Adds humor or uses double meanings.
    - “Soda Ban Goas Flat”
- **Sensationalism** - Dramatic and shocking.
    - “Shocking Truth Behind Celebrity Scandal”
- **Use of Number**s - Quantifiers. Gives credibility.
    - “15 Arrests in Crime Ring Bust”
- **Present Tense with Action Words** - Sense of immediacy.
    - “Storm Wreaks Havoc on Guerrero Shores”
- **Quotes and Direct Speech** - Interesting or controversial quote to entice readers
    - “I Will Not Resign! Prime Minister’s Response to Scandal”

# Types

- **Hard News** - Objective and factual. Reports on current events, politics, crime.
    - **Tone**: Neutral and formal.
    - **Example**: “Government Announces New Tax Reform”
- **Soft News** - Human interest, entertainment, culture, lifestyle, health.
    - **Tone**: Engaging and informal.
    - **Example**: “80-year-old Marathon Runner Defies Age”
- **Investigative Journalism** - In-depth critical reports that reveal hidden truths, or wrongdoings
    - **Tone**: Serious and analytical, sometimes accusatory.
    - **Example**: “Inside The Locker Room - Bullying and Harassment in Canada’s Hockey League”
- **Sensational News** - Exaggerated emotional, clickbait. Uses dramatic language and shock factor. Entertaining as well as factual.
    - Tone: Emotional and urgent.
    - **Example**: “STAY AT HOME! Coronavirus reaches millions”
- **Opinion Articles** - Subjective and persuasive. Writer expresses opinion.
    - **Tone**: Argumentative and persuasive, sometimes biased.
    - **Example**: “What Trudeau Should Do in Wake of Trump’s New Tariffs”
- **Propaganda** - Heavily influenced by political agendas, misleading and one-sided.
    - **Tone**: Persuasive.
    - **Examples**: “Why Our President is The Only Hope for Our Nation”
